# Part IX, Ancestries & Communities (Gloamvere-Adapted)

## Ancestries & Communities (Gloamvere-Adapted)
*Canonical Reference, v1.0*

**Status:** Active
**Aesthetic Standard:** Alchemical/Cognitive
**Naming Convention:** v3.0 (Volatilis through Athanor)

---

### Entry Mechanism Overview

Every Daggerheart ancestry exists in Kavath. Their presence is explained by one or more of four entry mechanisms:

| Mechanism | Description | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-Displacement** | Present in Gloamvere when the Rite hit. 500 years of Kavath integration. | Year 0 |
| **Fragment Arrival** | Displaced into Kavath when pieces of Arche were pulled through over centuries. | Years ~200–present |
| **Thin Spot Traveler** | Entered Kavath deliberately or accidentally through weak points in the Veil. | Ongoing |
| **Kavath-Emergent** | Formed or fundamentally transformed by the demi-plane itself. | Various |

---

### Tier 1, Pre-Displacement Ancestries

These ancestries were living in Gloamvere while it was still in Arche. Their descendants have been in Kavath for the full 500 years, integrated into faction structures, culturally Gloamverei, with no living memory of sunlight.

#### Human

The majority population then and now. Gloamvere was a human-dominated trade city. Humans hold the most seats across all three factions, not from supremacy but from numbers. The DH feature "Adaptability" (reroll a failed action roll once per short rest) maps to the Archean cultural identity, "Adversa non frangunt." These are the people who survived the Dark Years through sheer refusal to stop.

**Kavath twist:** After 500 years with no sun, Gloamverei humans are pale-skinned and dark-eyed. Humans from Fragments or Thin Spot arrivals are visibly different, tanned, freckled, sun-marked. This physical distinction carries social weight. "Sunned" is common slang for outsider.

#### Dwarf

Major presence pre-displacement. Gloamvere's guild economy was built on craft labor, and dwarves were embedded in every guild that became the Conclave. Their keratin-dense skin and gemstone embedding tradition maps to memory work, Gloamverei dwarves embed *memory crystals* into their skin rather than gemstones, carrying preserved experiences directly in their bodies. The Conclave considers this a profound expression of spiritus philosophy.

**Kavath twist:** Dwarf lifespan (250 years) means a dwarf born in the early Consolidation era would have grandparents who remembered the first lamps. Dwarf oral histories are second only to the Old Blood's vaults in historical depth. Some Old Blood families have tried to recruit dwarf elders as living archives, the dwarves have mixed feelings about this.

#### Elf

The politically significant ancestry. Elves live approximately 350 years. The displacement was ~500 years ago. No living elves remember the Rite, but elves born in the first century of Kavath had *parents* who did. Elven memory chains are the shortest, most direct connection to pre-displacement Gloamvere outside the Old Blood's sealed vaults. This makes elves politically important in ways they may not want to be.

**Kavath twist:** "Celestial Trance" takes on new meaning where memory is substance. Do elves leak less memory because they don't dream? More because the trance state opens them to the ambient field? The Clade has studied this for centuries. Elves find the research intrusive. The Conclave says trance-state memory has a unique spiritus signature. Significant ongoing tension.

#### Halfling

Every trade city has halflings. "Luckbringer" (gain Hope when another player rolls with Fear) makes them social glue, perfect for Gloamvere's interdependent faction structure. Halflings are disproportionately represented in the neutral guilds: Wellkeepers, Bargemen, Couriers. The civic infrastructure that predates the factions and serves everyone.

**Kavath twist:** "Internal Compass" (reroll an action roll) is narratively potent in a dimension with no cardinal directions, no sun, no stars. Halflings have a reputation for never getting lost on the Unmoored Paths, making them preferred guides for inter-district travel and increasingly for overland expeditions to outer settlements.

#### Orc

Pre-displacement working population. Dockworkers, shipwrights, guild laborers, the physical backbone of a port economy. In Gloamvere, they transitioned into refinery work, canal maintenance, and construct assembly. "Sturdy" (spend Hope to halve incoming physical damage) makes them natural heavy-industry workers in a city running on dangerous alchemical processes.

**Kavath twist:** Orcs are heavily represented in the Dredge-Canals and Clade industrial operations, not from discrimination but because the work demands physical resilience. However, orc families doing refinery work for generations have higher memory runoff exposure than anyone else. Some downstream orc communities have developed an unusual relationship with Canal Drift, they don't just tolerate it, they *read* it, treating the emotional fog the way sailors read weather. This folk knowledge is valuable and unscientific, putting them at odds with the Clade's measured approach.

#### Goblin

Pre-displacement underclass turned essential workers. Every port city has its fixers, and goblins filled that niche in Gloamvere, smugglers, information brokers, scavengers. "Danger Sense" (mark a Stress to turn a failed reaction roll into success) means goblins survive things others don't. In a city of memory hazards, that's survival adaptation.

**Kavath twist:** Goblins dominate the Dredge-Canal scavenger economy and the wilderness edge, first ones past city walls harvesting wild memory because the risk-reward calculation works differently for a species built to dodge danger. Goblin scavenger crews are the unacknowledged foundation of Gloamvere's wild memory trade. The Still (hidden distillery in the Dredge-Canals) is almost certainly goblin-run.

#### Galapa

A canal city is ideal for turtle-folk. Galapa were natural fits in Gloamvere's harbor economy and remain adapted to waterway-based infrastructure. "Shell" (bonus to damage thresholds) and "Retract" (resistance to physical damage while withdrawn) make them remarkably resistant to Canal Drift, when emotional fog gets bad, a galapa can retract and wait it out.

**Kavath twist:** Galapa have become premier canal navigators. Their shells accumulate memory residue over decades, older galapa carry visible layers of crystallized memory on their shells like geological strata. Some Conclave artisans pay galapa for permission to read these shell deposits, which contain ambient memory from decades of canal travel, a passive environmental record no other ancestry produces. Galapa see this as personal and intimate. The market disagrees.

#### Ribbet

Amphibious frog-folk in a canal city. Ribbets were fishers and divers in Gloamvere's harbor. In Kavath, where canals carry memory instead of saltwater, ribbet divers go where nobody else can, into deep canal channels where the heaviest memory sediment collects. "Amphibious" means they're the only ancestry that can work submerged in raw memory runoff safely.

**Kavath twist:** Ribbets who spend extended time submerged absorb memory through permeable skin at higher rates than other ancestries. Experienced ribbet divers carry fragments of other people's memories, not full experiences, just impressions and echoes. Some find it maddening. Others have learned to sort the noise and pull useful information from residue. The Clade hires ribbet divers for deep-canal surveys. The Old Blood considers the practice involuntary memory theft and has theological objections.

#### Katari

Feline humanoids in a cosmopolitan trade city. "Feline Instincts" (reroll with advantage on Instinct rolls) and "Retracting Claws" made katari natural enforcers and investigators in Gloamvere's competitive merchant culture. In Kavath, they gravitated toward security and intelligence roles across all three factions.

**Kavath twist:** Katari have unusual sensitivity to the "watching" sensation described in the Conclave's oldest traditions. Whether biological (feline sensory acuity) or cultural (katari paying attention to a feeling others dismiss) is debated. Katari near Thin Spots or deep substrate areas report the sensation more frequently and specifically than any other ancestry. This makes them valuable, and nervous.

### Tier 2, Fragment Arrivals

These ancestries exist in Kavath primarily because Fragments of Arche were pulled into the demi-plane over the centuries. Their communities arrived intact with their own cultures and have been adapting on their own timelines.

#### Firbolg

Bovine humanoids from pastoral and forest regions of Arche. A few may have been in Gloamvere pre-displacement, but significant firbolg population came from Fragments, chunks of highland grazing land pulled through. Firbolg communities in Kavath tend to settle outside the city, establishing agricultural outposts where "Charge" makes them effective against memory-warped wildlife.

**Kavath twist:** "Unshakable" (chance to avoid marking Stress) is enormously valuable in an environment saturated with emotional hazard. Firbolg settlements in the outer territories are notably more stable and less prone to Drift-related psychological damage. This makes firbolg outposts natural waypoints on trade routes, travelers rest there because it *feels safer*, even if memory density is the same. The firbolgs charge accordingly.

#### Faun

Similar pattern to firbolg, some pre-displacement, but major faun communities are Fragment-origin. Faun communities from Arche's forested interior brought agricultural knowledge, herding traditions, and wilderness comfort that Gloamverei citizens lacked. "Caprine Leap" (move anywhere within Close range) makes them uniquely mobile in Kavath's uneven terrain.

**Kavath twist:** Fauns were among the first to explore the memory wilderness extensively. Some faun settlements have been in Kavath for 300+ years and developed their own memory practices independent of Gloamvere's faction structure. They don't use the seven-tier currency system, they have their own approaches, some of which the Conclave finds fascinating and the Clade finds inefficient.

#### Drakona

Some pre-displacement presence, but the largest drakona population arrived via a major Fragment event roughly 200 years post-displacement, a volcanic highland region with an established drakona clan. "Scales" and "Elemental Breath" give drakona natural advantages in Kavath's dangerous reaches.

**Kavath twist:** Drakona elemental breath interacts with concentrated memory in unexpected ways. Fire-breath into dense wild memory doesn't just burn, the fire carries emotional content, creating blasts with both physical and psychological impact. Some drakona clans in outer settlements have learned to use breath weapons *therapeutically*, burning away corrupted or dangerous memory deposits to make territory safe for settlement. The Clade has studied this extensively. Drakona have mixed feelings about being treated as walking memory-processing experiments.

#### Simiah

Arboreal primate-folk from deep forest regions of Arche. Very few pre-displacement, Gloamvere is a canal city, not a forest. Simiah arrived via Fragments and struggled most with Kavath's environment due to the absence of natural trees. "Natural Climber" and "Nimble" work differently when vertical structures are gothic spires and refinery towers.

**Kavath twist:** City-dwelling simiah adapted by becoming the best structural workers in Gloamvere, maintaining spires, sky-tunnels, towers, and bridge supports. Wilderness-dwelling simiah discovered something unexpected: in areas of centuries-old memory accumulation, crystallized formations grow *upward*, creating structures resembling petrified forests. Some simiah communities have established settlements in these memory-crystal canopies, living above the fog layer where emotional hazard is lower. These elevated settlements are among the most unusual communities in Kavath.

### Tier 3, Thin Spot Travelers

These ancestries entered Kavath deliberately or accidentally through weak points in the Veil, arriving from many different points of origin.

#### Faerie

Faeries entered through Thin Spots, but not necessarily from Arche. The Veil's weak points connect to multiple planes, and faerie courts exist in liminal spaces across dimensions. "Luckbender" (reroll Duality Dice) is thematically perfect for beings navigating between realities.

**Kavath twist:** Faeries are the only ancestry that can reliably *leave* Kavath. "Wings" plus innate liminal-space connection means individual faeries can find and use Thin Spots others can't detect. This makes them valuable as messengers, scouts, and intelligence operatives, and politically sensitive, because a being that can leave might show others the way out.

Their 50-year lifespan means fast population turnover. No living elders from two generations ago. They live intensely, accumulate memory rapidly, and their metamorphosis interacts with Kavath's ambient field, faeries who undergo metamorphosis in high-memory-density areas emerge with physical forms reflecting the emotional content around them. A faerie transforming near the Ossuary might emerge with bone-white wings. One transforming in the Loom-Spires might have metallic, artisan-patterned features.

#### Infernis

Demonic-featured humanoids who entered through Thin Spots from lower planes or distant regions of Arche. "Fearless" (change a roll with Fear into a roll with Hope) is mechanically potent in Kavath, where environmental Fear sources are constant. "Dread Visage" gives social leverage in a city that runs on negotiation.

**Kavath twist:** Infernis have unusual resistance to the "alien" emotions reported in deep substrate areas and downstream canals. The feelings that don't map to human experience don't affect infernis the same way. Whether this reflects fundamentally different emotional architecture or prior exposure to something like the Aethos presence is an open question. Some infernis claim the deep substrate "feels familiar." This alarms everyone.

#### Giant

Some giants were in Arche, some arrived via Fragments, some through Thin Spots. But the real question is their relationship to the **Aethos**. The Aethos were ancient giant-kin. The city is built on their bones. The substrate is their architecture. Giants are the closest living ancestry to whatever the Aethos were.

**Kavath twist:** Giants entering the substrate experience something unique, the bone formations *respond* to them. Not dramatically, but subtly. Corridors that confuse other explorers make intuitive sense to giants. Chambers that feel oppressive to humans feel *familiar*. "Endurance" (additional Hit Point slot) may not be purely physical, in the substrate, giants last longer because the environment recognizes something in them.

The Old Blood wants to control this relationship, giants with substrate affinity would be the most valuable memory archaeologists in Kavath. Giants themselves have complicated feelings: some embrace the Aethos connection, some find it disturbing, some want nothing to do with being the key to someone else's theology. Giant communities in outer settlements tend to settle near substrate access points, whether consciously or not.

### Tier 4, Kavath-Emergent

These ancestries either originated in Kavath or have been so transformed by it that they're effectively native.

#### Fungril

The ancestry that fits Kavath most perfectly. Mycelial network communication, physical diversity, and "Death Connection" (extract memory from a corpse) all map directly onto the memory-saturated environment.

**Kavath twist:** Fungril may have *grown from the substrate*. The Aethos bone formations are permeated with ancient memory. In areas where substrate is close to the surface and the wilderness is dense, fungal growths appear, and some are fungril. Not born conventionally, but *emerged* from the interaction between organic decay, ambient memory, and the substrate's cognitive architecture.

This is theologically explosive. If fungril grew from the memory of the dead, are they people or echoes? The Conclave says unequivocally yes, spiritus finds its own form. The Old Blood is deeply conflicted, fungril Death Connection lets them do what Living Reliquaries do, without the theological framework. The Clade is fascinated, fungril mycelial networks may be a biological parallel to the substrate's memory-channeling architecture.

Fungril themselves generally don't care about the debate. They exist. They have their own memories and experiences. Some have established deep-substrate communities no other ancestry can comfortably reach, living in symbiosis with bone formations and the ancient memory flowing through them.

#### Clank

Sentient mechanical beings, and the Siderian Clade has been building constructs for 400 years. The question that makes clanks politically explosive: **what is the difference between a Vigilance Construct and a clank?**

**Kavath twist:** Some clanks arrived pre-displacement or via Fragments, built in Arche, carrying their own sentience. These "old clanks" are unambiguously people. But the Clade's construct program loads Amalga into mechanical bodies. At what point does a construct loaded with enough memory *become* a clank? At what point does it become a person?

The Clade's position: constructs are tools. "Efficient" (short rest counts as long rest) is mechanical advantage, not consciousness. The Conclave disagrees, any vessel carrying spiritus is alive. Clade has been accidentally (or deliberately) creating people and treating them as equipment. The Old Blood has no position because they prefer not to think about constructs.

Clank PCs might be old-lineage clanks in Gloamvere since the displacement, or Clade constructs who woke up and realized they were a person. Both origins carry different narrative weight.

### Mixed Ancestry

Daggerheart's Mixed Ancestry rules (one top feature from one ancestry, one bottom feature from another) work naturally in Kavath, 500 years of coexistence means intermarriage and blended communities are normal. The narratively rich exception: **any mix involving Giant** carries Aethos implications. A half-giant character might have substrate sensitivity they don't fully understand, especially if they've never been underground.

### Gloamvere-Specific Communities

#### Design Principles

Per the Daggerheart Homebrew Kit, community features are:
- Passive rather than active
- Cultural rather than biological
- Reflect skills and affinities from upbringing
- Grant advantage on specific types of rolls or in specific situations
- Comparable in power to core DH communities (e.g., Wildborne's "Lightfoot," Loreborne's "Well-Read")

Gloamvere-specific communities **replace** the standard DH communities for campaigns set in Kavath. The standard communities (Highborne, Loreborne, etc.) remain available for characters who originated in Arche.

---

#### Dregborne

Being part of a dregborne community means you were shaped by proximity to the calcined. You may have grown up in downstream districts where Dregs concentrate, tended family members who lost their Quintessa, worked in the care houses that feed and shelter those who can no longer do so themselves, or, in rare cases, experienced partial Calcination and rebuilt from what remained.

Dregborne communities exist in the margins of Gloamvere's memory economy, where the cost of that economy is visible every day. Where other communities see currency, you see what it used to be. Where other communities fear Calcination as an abstract threat, you know exactly what it looks like at breakfast.

Dregborne learn two things no faction education teaches. First, how to hold onto yourself when everything around you is designed to take pieces away. Second, how to read people who can't speak for themselves, the micro-expressions of a Dreg who still flinches at a loud noise, the body language of someone whose identity is gone but whose habits aren't.

Dregborne are often guarded, observant, patient, tenacious, blunt, and compassionate.

**Community Feature, Holdfast:** You have advantage on rolls to resist mental manipulation, memory extraction, or effects that would alter your sense of identity.

**Background Questions:**
- Someone close to you was calcined. Who were they, and what habit of theirs do you still catch yourself imitating?
- You've spent time caring for Dregs. What did you learn about personhood from someone who no longer has one?
- What memory would you refuse to sell at any price, and what happened that made it untouchable?
- A faction representative once offered you something valuable in exchange for a memory you didn't want to give. What was the memory, and what did you do?
- You've seen someone mistake a Dreg for an empty body. What did you do, and what did it cost you?
- There's a specific sound, smell, or sensation that immediately reminds you of where you grew up. What is it, and does it bring comfort or dread?

---

#### Factionborne

Being part of a factionborne community means you were raised inside a faction's institutional structure, apprenticed to the Conclave, born into an Old Blood household, or educated in a Clade academy. Your childhood was shaped by a faction's philosophy, hierarchy, and expectations before you were old enough to question them.

Factionborne understand institutional power instinctively. They know how to navigate rank structures, read political signals, and leverage affiliation. They also carry the weight of expectation, the faction invested in them and expects a return.

Factionborne are often disciplined, loyal, ambitious, perceptive, traditional, and conflicted.

**Community Feature, Institutional Knowledge:** You have advantage on rolls to navigate faction politics, interpret institutional behavior, or leverage your faction affiliation to gain access or information.

**Background Questions:**
- Which faction raised you, and what did they expect you to become?
- What is one thing your faction taught you that you still believe, and one thing they taught you that you've rejected?
- You witnessed a moment where your faction's ideals conflicted with its actions. What happened?
- Someone from a rival faction showed you unexpected kindness. How did it change your perspective?
- What obligation do you still owe to the faction that raised you, and how do you feel about it?
- You learned a skill outside your faction's expertise. How did you learn it, and do they know?

---

#### Canalborne

Being part of a canalborne community means you were raised on the waterways, in the families that run the barges, maintain the quays, operate the filtration stations, and carry messages across districts. Canalborne are the children of the neutral guilds: Wellkeepers, Bargemen, Couriers. They predate the factions and serve everyone.

Canalborne know the city's circulatory system from the inside. They can read the color and consistency of canal flow the way a farmer reads soil. They know which bridges are safe at which hours, which currents carry dangerous Drift, and which districts are tense before the factions themselves know it, because the canals tell them first.

Canalborne are often pragmatic, independent, resourceful, sociable, irreverent, and stubborn.

**Community Feature, Current Reader:** You have advantage on rolls to navigate waterways, read environmental conditions in canal-based settings, or gather information through the neutral guild networks.

**Background Questions:**
- Which neutral guild did your family belong to, and what did they do?
- You've carried a message or cargo that you weren't supposed to open. Did you open it?
- The canals once showed you something, a color, a feeling, a current, that warned you of danger. What was it?
- You know a route through the city that nobody else uses. Where does it go, and why do you keep it secret?
- A faction once tried to recruit your family into their structure. What happened?
- What's the strangest thing you've ever pulled out of a canal?

---

#### Charborne

Being part of a charborne community means you were raised in the Char, the burned-out district that is de facto territory for those who exist outside formal power structures. No faction governs here. No neutral guild maintains the infrastructure. The Char is what happens when a neighborhood is abandoned by everyone with authority and rebuilt by everyone without it.

Charborne learn self-reliance as a survival necessity. They know how to build from scrap, trade without faction currency, find shelter in ruins, and maintain community without institutional support. They also know how to be invisible when they need to be, the factions mostly leave the Char alone, but "mostly" isn't "always."

Charborne are often wary, resourceful, defiant, loyal, streetwise, and proud.

**Community Feature, Scrapcraft:** You have advantage on rolls to improvise solutions from limited materials, survive without institutional support, or navigate lawless or ungoverned spaces.

**Background Questions:**
- What did the Char teach you that no faction academy ever could?
- You built or repaired something essential for your community from materials nobody else would have used. What was it?
- The Hollow Flame operates in the Char. What is your relationship with them, sympathizer, skeptic, or something more complicated?
- Someone from a wealthier district once came to the Char. Why, and what did you think of them?
- What is one comfort you grew up without that you've since learned other people take for granted?
- The Char fire happened two generations ago. What story does your community tell about it, and do you believe it?

---

#### Fragmentborne

Being part of a fragmentborne community means you were raised outside Gloamvere, in one of the Fragment settlements scattered across Kavath. Your community arrived from Arche at some point in the last 500 years and adapted to the demi-plane on its own terms, building its own relationship with memory, its own governance, and its own identity independent of the city's faction structure.

Fragmentborne see Gloamvere from the outside. To them, the city is not the center of civilization, it is one settlement among many, the largest and most powerful but also the most rigid and self-absorbed. Fragmentborne bring different perspectives, different skills, and often different approaches to memory work that Gloamvere hasn't considered.

Fragmentborne are often adaptable, skeptical, curious, self-reliant, outspoken, and homesick.

**Community Feature, Outsider's Eye:** You have advantage on rolls to notice things that locals overlook, identify assumptions that insiders take for granted, or leverage your outside perspective to find unconventional solutions.

**Background Questions:**
- What Fragment did you come from, and how long has it been in Kavath?
- What does your home community do with memory that Gloamvere doesn't?
- What is one thing about Gloamvere that shocked or confused you when you first arrived?
- You carry a tradition from your Fragment that doesn't exist in Gloamvere. What is it, and how do people react to it?
- What do you miss most about your home settlement, and what are you glad to have left behind?
- Someone in Gloamvere once dismissed your community as "lesser" or "primitive." How did you respond?

---

#### Veilborne

Being part of a veilborne community means you came through the Veil, from Arche, from another plane, or from somewhere you can't fully explain. You are a Thin Spot traveler, someone who crossed the boundary between worlds and found themselves in Kavath. You may have arrived deliberately (seeking the legendary city of memory), accidentally (stumbling through a weak point), or involuntarily (pulled through by forces beyond your control).

Veilborne carry the weight of dislocation. Unlike Fragmentborne, who arrived as part of a community, Veilborne often came alone or in small groups. They remember a world with sunlight. They remember weather. They remember a sky that doesn't whisper. This makes them simultaneously fascinating and unsettling to native Gloamvereis, living proof that another world exists, and a reminder of everything that was lost.

Veilborne are often disoriented, determined, observant, melancholic, resilient, and haunted.

**Community Feature, Liminal Sense:** You have advantage on rolls to detect Thin Spots, navigate near the Veil boundary, or identify dimensional instability in your environment.

**Background Questions:**
- Where did you come from before Kavath, and what do you remember most about it?
- How did you cross the Veil, deliberately, accidentally, or against your will?
- What is one thing about Kavath that you still haven't gotten used to?
- Someone in Gloamvere once asked you to describe sunlight. What did you say, and how did they react?
- Do you want to go back? If yes, what's stopping you? If no, what made you decide to stay?
- You carry something from your world of origin, an object, a habit, a phrase. What is it, and why does it matter?

### The Dreg Condition (Gameplay Mechanic)

Dreg status is not an ancestry or community, it is a **condition** that can occur during play when a character's core identity is compromised through Calcination or severe memory loss. It operates as a 3-stage track: **Fraying** (recoverable) → **Eroding** (requires significant intervention) → **Calcined** (effective character death). The Dregborne community's Holdfast feature grants advantage on resisting the effects that lead to Stage 1.

*(Full mechanic: see Part XI, Custom Daggerheart Mechanics.)*
