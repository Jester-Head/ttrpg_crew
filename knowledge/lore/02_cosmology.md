### The Material Plane: Arche

Arche is the world from which Gloamvere City was displaced. It is a Material Plane with standard physics, sunlight, weather, oceans, seasons, diverse biomes, and multiple civilizations. Gloamvere City sat on a cluster of lagoon islands at the mouth of the Vastus River, controlling access between inland trade routes and maritime commerce. It was "the bottleneck of the world's economy", a phrase that only makes sense if there was a world-scale economy flowing through it.

#### The Region Around Gloamvere City

The full scope of Arche does not need to be mapped. What matters is enough geography to give every Daggerheart ancestry a plausible point of origin and enough structure to support plot hooks involving the Material Plane.

**Upriver, The Vastus Hinterlands.** Agricultural plains, mountain passes, and mining communities along the Vastus River and its tributaries. Raw materials flowed downriver to Gloamvere City for processing and trade. This is where dwarves, humans, and orcs had deep roots, mining settlements, farming communities, and river-trade towns that fed the city's economy.

**Coastal Regions.** Fishing communities, port towns, and naval traditions along the coastline surrounding Gloamvere City's lagoon. Galapa, ribbets, and seaborne cultures thrived here. The Silver Bellies calamity (beached sea life, plague, harbor blockage) affected the entire coast, not just Gloamvere City.

**Forested Interior.** Deep old-growth wilderness away from the river trade routes. Firbolg, faun, elf, and fungril territory. These communities traded with Gloamvere City but were not dependent on it, they had their own economies, their own cultures, and their own relationship with the land.

**The Aethos Ranges.** Mountain or highland region where the giant-kin Aethos originally lived before their civilization fell. Giants still inhabit these areas in the present day. The burial mounds that became Gloamvere City's foundation were an extension of this territory, the Aethos buried their dead on the coastal islands the way some cultures use offshore sacred sites.

**Other Nations.** Gloamvere City was a trade hub, not an empire. Multiple sovereign states, kingdoms, and independent communities sent merchants through the city. Drakona clans, infernis enclaves, katari nomads, simiah arboreal cities, faerie courts, all existed in Arche with varying relationships to Gloamvere City's economy.

#### Post-Displacement Arche

When Gloamvere City vanished, it left a hole. The lagoon islands are simply gone. A void in the water where a city used to be. The Vastus River now empties into a bay that shouldn't exist. The surrounding region experienced economic collapse (their trade bottleneck disappeared overnight), followed by slow recovery over centuries.

Some communities near the former site still experience Veil bleedthrough, strange moods, memory distortions, a persistent sense of something missing. The dimensional scar left by the Rite of Unmaking has not fully healed after 500 years. This bleedthrough is one mechanism by which people in Arche occasionally stumble into Kavath through Thin Spots.

#### Design Principle

Arche is a full world, but only the region around Gloamvere City's former location needs detail now. Distant continents, other civilizations, and deep world history can be filled in as needed, when a specific ancestry requires an origin point, when a campaign requires a Material Plane location, or when the setting needs to grow. The structure supports expansion without requiring it.

### The Demi-Plane: Kavath

Kavath is not an empty pocket dimension just large enough to hold one city. It is a region, alien, memory-saturated, and largely unexplored, with three distinct strata that layer together to create a varied environment.

#### The Three Strata

##### Stratum 1, The Surface: Memory Wilderness

This is what you see when you leave **Gloamvere City's** outermost districts and walk past the last bridge. The terrain is real, ground, formations that function like hills and valleys, bodies of prismatic liquid, but it is shaped by five centuries of uncontained memory accumulating with no one to harvest it.

**Near the city edge:** Thin wilderness. Ambient **memory vapor** creates mild *Drift* effects, stray feelings, brief sensory flashes, emotional bleed. The ground is dusted with fine crystalline residue. Scavengers from the Dredge-Canals venture here to collect raw memory for resale. Dangerous but known territory.

**Further out:** Memory density increases. Without human curation, the substance compounds, layers of leaked experience pressing down on older layers, fusing, interacting. The landscape reflects its contents:
- A valley where centuries of grief pooled: permanently shrouded in fog that makes travelers weep without knowing why.
- A ridge where discarded rage accumulated: jagged red-black crystal formations radiating hostility.
- A depression where joy concentrated: the one place in Kavath that feels warm and bright, and the most dangerous, because people who find it don't want to leave.

**The far reaches:** The terrain stops resembling anything human. The memory here predates the city's five centuries, either older layers of accumulated cognition, Aethos bleedthrough from below, or something else entirely. The Clade's survey expeditions have mapped perhaps ten percent of the accessible surface. The rest is unknown.

**What lives here:** Memory-warped fauna. Not displaced creatures, creatures that *formed* from concentrated memory the way crystals form from saturated solution. A mass of accumulated predatory instinct that moves like a wolf but has no biology. A cloud of condensed loneliness that follows travelers and slowly drains their will to continue. Daggerheart adversaries can be reskinned: elementals as memory constructs, beasts as instinct-formations.

**What's valuable here:** Raw memory deposits. Gloamvere City's economy runs on extracted human memory, but the wilderness contains *wild* memory, unextracted, unrefined, often ancient. Expeditions to harvest wild memory are lucrative and dangerous. The Clade funds most of them. The Conclave considers wild memory spiritually compromised. The Old Blood considers it theologically irrelevant because it was never properly preserved.

> **Terminology note (Drift):** 
> **Drift** = the symptomset (emotional bleed, sensory flashes, intrusive affect). 
> **Canal Drift** = Drift caused by refinement runoff within the city's waterways. 
> **Wild Drift** = Drift caused by ambient memory saturation in the wilderness.
> **Environmental Drift** = accelerated degradation of carried memories near Thin Spots and on Unmoored Paths.

##### Stratum 2, The Fragments: Displaced Communities

Scattered across the memory wilderness like islands in a strange sea are the **Fragments**, pieces of other places pulled into Kavath.

The Rite of Unmaking didn't make a clean cut. It ripped Gloamvere City out of Arche, but the boundary aftershock did not stabilize cleanly. Over the years and centuries that followed, smaller pieces of the Material Plane slipped through, a village, a stretch of farmland, occasionally just a building standing alone with no context.

**How Fragments arrive (taxonomy):**
- **Thin Spots (threshold-scale):** Structured, liminal faults (doorframes, archways, open graves, empty rooms). Traversable passages. Intention-reactive. These are "doors."
- **Scars (area-scale):** Larger, unstable boundary failures, shears rather than doors. Scars can take *chunks* (fields, blocks, villages) and leave behind lasting hazard-zones on both sides. Scars are rarer, messier, and harder to predict.

**Established settlements.** Some Fragments arrived centuries ago with enough people and resources to survive. They discovered memory-as-substance independently, developed their own extraction methods, built their own cultures around it. They may have active trade relationships with Gloamvere City or may have never made contact. Some are friendly, some hostile, some indifferent. Some have developed memory technology the city hasn't thought of, because they started from different cultural foundations.

**Recent arrivals.** Newer Fragments are disoriented, terrified, and resource-poor, exactly like Gloamvere City during the Dark Years. The factions argue about response: the Conclave tends toward assistance, the Old Blood toward assessment of lineage value, the Clade toward resource calculation.

**Dead Fragments.** Not every displaced community survived. The wilderness contains ruins of settlements that didn't make it, ghost towns where the memory of their failed inhabitants still lingers in the walls. Exploration sites, loot sources, cautionary tales.

**Non-human Fragments.** Not everything pulled into Kavath came from the region around Gloamvere City. Pieces of distant Arche, or other planes entirely, have arrived carrying people, creatures, and cultures Gloamvere City has no frame of reference for. A Fragment carrying a firbolg forest community. A drakona clan displaced from a volcanic range on another continent. A faerie court that slipped through from a connected fey dimension. Each Fragment arrival is a potential story hook, ancestry introduction, and new community to interact with.

**What connects the Fragments:** The wilderness between them is navigable but not safe. Trade routes between Gloamvere City and larger established settlements exist, maintained by bargemen and couriers who've learned to read emotional terrain the way sailors read currents.

##### Stratum 3, The Deep: The Aethos Substrate

Beneath everything, beneath the wilderness, beneath the Fragments, beneath Gloamvere City itself, the bones go down.

Gloamvere City has always known about Aethos formations in its foundations. What is disputed is the **extent** and **origin** of what lies below.

**Clade hypothesis (dominant):** The Aethos tumuli imported with the city have **fused with** or **interface with** a wider subterranean structure in Kavath. In this model, "Aethos substrate" is a hybrid: partly imported remains, partly Kavath-native framework, transformed by Veil exposure and long-term cognitive saturation.

**Old Blood doctrine (simplified):** The bones are the bones; what lies below is sacred burial made deeper by time and sin.

**Conclave teaching model (private):** The deep is "where spiritus learns to move." Whether that movement is natural, engineered, or watched is not discussed outside master lineage.

The substrate is simultaneously skeletal ruin, architectural presence, and geological foundation. Massive calcified ribs curve into cathedral-sized vaults. Corridors of mineralized bone connect chambers shaped by intelligent hands. The deeper you go, the more intentional the construction becomes, upper layers look like natural formations; lower layers are unmistakably engineered.

**Memory behaves differently underground.** On the surface, wild memory accumulates passively. In the deep, it appears to **flow**, channeled through corridors, pooling in specific chambers, cycling in patterns that suggest design. If this behavior predates human arrival, then Gloamvere City's memory economy may be a crude surface-level imitation of a system the deep has run for far longer than five centuries.

**What's down there (contested but recurring reports):**
- **Aethos memory.** Raw cognition that does not map cleanly to human emotion. The "alien impressions" reported in the deepest Dredge-Canals are commonly treated as bleedthrough from below.
- **Infrastructure.** Chambers, corridors, mechanisms, some still functioning. The Clade has found rooms where memory is automatically sorted by type. Other chambers amplify emotional content to overwhelming levels. Others nullify memory entirely, walk in with a full vial, walk out with an empty one and no recollection of its contents.
- **Persistence.** Not "living Aethos" in a mortal sense, but the possibility of **geological-scale cognitive presence** embedded in structure, slow, vast, and incompatible with human perception.

#### How the Three Strata Interact

The deep leaks upward. Substrate bleedthrough influences surface terrain, the oldest, strangest features correlate with major deep structures. The Clade has noticed the correlation but cannot fully explain it.

Fragments that land near deep access points have an advantage, they can tap deep behavior for memory processing, giving them a technological head start. Some established Fragment settlements may have built economies around deep structures that differ from Gloamvere City's approach.

Gloamvere City itself sits on one of the densest junctions, the Ossuary Isles were Aethos tumuli, meaning the city was built on a major node. This may be why the Rite of Unmaking behaved the way it did: the tear occurred where the boundary between surface life and deep structure was already thin and saturated.

**Conclave metaphor (teaching, not proof):** 
"The Veil is the skin, the wilderness is the flesh, and the deep is the bone." 
Whether that "body" belongs to something alive is the Nyxentia question, scaled to planar proportions.

### The Veil

The Veil is the semi-permeable boundary enclosing **Kavath** (not just Gloamvere City). It is visible from Gloamvere City's outermost districts as an iridescent shimmer at the edge of sight. Five hundred years of observation have not resolved its nature.

#### Interaction Rules (Hard Canon)

- **Raw Veil contact:** does not return. The unstructured boundary is lethal/unrecoverable.
- **Thin Spot traversal:** structured passage through a threshold-scale fault. These behave like doors.
- **Preservation rites:** are performed **at Thin Spots** (or constructed liminal sites), not by "touching the Veil."

#### Observations (Documented; Meaning Disputed)

**Physical properties:** The Veil weakens at thresholds, doorframes, archways, open graves, empty rooms, creating Thin Spots where planar travelers enter and exit, where Preservation rituals are performed, and where boundary effects are most pronounced. It responds to *intention*. The same Thin Spots used for Preservation rituals are also the points where the Veil is thinnest.

**The whispers:** The sky whispers. Every faction has documented it. The Old Blood says regrets of the living reflecting off the Veil's inner surface. The Clade says sub-threshold auditory hallucinations from ambient memory vapor interacting with the cochlear nerve. The occasional reports of words forming in an unknown language are filed, noted, and not discussed in polite company.

**Canal anomalies:** Downstream, where memory runoff concentrates, people occasionally report emotional impressions that don't match any known human experience. These reports are rare, difficult to verify, and easy to dismiss as Drift symptoms. They persist across five centuries of documentation.

**The watching:** The Conclave's oldest oral tradition. Not written down, not shared with outsiders: the spiritus sometimes behaves as though it is being observed. Not all the time. Not in any pattern. But occasionally, when working with memory at its most sensitive, something *attends*.

### How Memory Became Substance

The displacement saturated Kavath's atmosphere with cognitive substance. Memory, uncontained and unprocessed, condensed into canals and air the way dew collects on cold glass.

The mechanism remains an open cosmological question, whether it is a natural property of Kavath, an effect of the Rite of Unmaking, a consequence of deep-structure behavior, or something deliberately engineered by whatever the Veil is.

What is established:
- Memory leaks from inhabitants involuntarily in Kavath's atmosphere
- The leakage can be controlled through deliberate extraction
- The extracted substance has measurable, classifiable properties (the seven-tier system)
- The substance can be used: burned for light, inhaled for experience transfer, infused into objects for enchantment
- Deep-structure behavior suggests this phenomenon may predate human arrival, or at minimum operates independently of human intent
