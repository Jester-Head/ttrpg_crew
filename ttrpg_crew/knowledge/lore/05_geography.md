# Part V, Gloamvere Geography & Districts

## Gloamvere Geography & Districts Reference Document
*Canonical Reference, v1.1*

**Status:** Active
**Aesthetic Standard:** Alchemical/Cognitive
**Naming Convention:** v3.0 (Volatilis through Athanor)

---

### Overview

Gloamvere is a canal city. Its districts are islands and peninsulas separated by waterways that no longer carry salt water but instead flow with the prismatic, semi-liquid runoff of memory distillation, bright and opalescent where the refineries discharge, settling into a dull mercury-like sludge in the downstream reaches where contextless feelings collect like sediment. The canals are the circulatory system of the memory economy made visible. To read the color of the current is to know where you stand.

The city is built from white stone darkened by age and industrial residue, crossed by humpbacked cobblestone bridges that connect neighborhoods at odd angles. Gothic spires rise above refinery smokestacks that vent thin plumes of memory vapor, the evaporated remnants of Volatilis that escaped containment during processing. On still days the vapor settles into a low fog that clings to the canal surfaces and carries faint, uninvited feelings: a pang of someone else's nostalgia, a flash of anger that isn't yours. Gloamvereis call this **Canal Drift** and treat it the way a port city treats sea spray, an annoyance, a fact of life, occasionally dangerous.

The skyline is a jagged silhouette of merchant spires, cathedral domes, brass-capped refinery towers, and the skeletal geometry of Clade sky-tunnels, all reflected in the shifting prismatic current. There is no sun. There is no weather. The sky is a permanent bruised purple, and the city's light comes from its own industry, the amber glow of Conclave-crafted fixtures, the cold blue-white of stabilized Precipia lamps, and the dim prismatic flicker of Leached dregs burning in the cheapest districts.

Sound carries across the canals. The din of hammer on anvil from a Conclave forge carries across three bridges. The low-frequency chanting of the Reminiscent drones outward from the Ossuary islands like a tide. The whir of Clade constructs on patrol echoes off canal walls. Beneath it all, the canals themselves make noise, a slow, thick lapping against stone quays that doesn't sound like anything natural, because it isn't.

#### The Core Principle

Gloamvere mourns its reality but refuses to yield. The people of this city were torn from Arche, from sunlight, oceans, weather, and deposited in Kavath, a demi-plane where memory became substance. They built on whatever ground the displacement gave them, in a place that shouldn't sustain life. Every bridge, every quay, every smoking refinery is an act of stubborn refusal to disappear.

#### Canal Geography

The city is best understood by its relationship to the current:

**Upstream Districts**: Closer to the refinery discharge points where memory is processed and the canal flow runs brightest. These neighborhoods are wealthier, better maintained, and saturated with ambient memory of higher quality. The air is cleaner but carries a faint chemical tang from stabilization compounds. Rank 4-5 faction members tend to maintain residences upstream, though they travel throughout the city.

**Midstream Districts**: The working city. Workshops, markets, temples, barracks, taverns, residences. Most of Gloamvere's population lives midstream, where the canals carry a mix of processed and raw memory and the bridges are busiest. Wrights, Reliquarians, Observers, Factors, the rank 2-3 backbone of every faction, work the midstream neighborhoods.

**Downstream Districts**: Where the heavy Leached runoff settles. The current here is thick, grey, and barely luminous. The buildings are older, less maintained, and the fog is denser because the low-grade memory vapor has nowhere to go. Remnants, the Bound, Novitiates, and those who have fallen out of faction structures populate the downstream reaches.

#### A Note on the Foundations

In places where the canals run deepest, particularly in the downstream reaches and beneath the Grand Rib-Market, the city's excavations have exposed ancient calcified structures of unknown origin. Massive curved formations of minerite that resemble ribs or vertebrae are embedded in the bedrock, predating any known construction. The Clade classifies these as geological formations from the pre-displacement substrate. The Old Blood calls them the bones of the Aethos and considers them sacred. The Conclave says they hum faintly when touched, which suggests residual spiritus. No faction has adequately explained what they are. Builders have learned to anchor foundations into them because the material is extraordinarily strong, and the Grand Rib-Market takes its name from the exposed formations visible in its lower levels. Beyond that, the Aethos remains an open question, not a defining feature of the city, but an unsettling reminder that Gloamvere was built on something that was here before.

### Faction Territories

#### 1. The Loom-Spires, Spiritus Conclave Territory

> *"Every crafted thing remembers. Our work is to make sure it remembers well."*

The Loom-Spires take their name from the district's oldest structures, a cluster of repurposed merchant towers fitted with the massive vertical looms of the Conclave's original weaving tradition. But the name has become a misnomer. The Conclave is no longer just a weaver's guild; it is a district of forges, carpentry shops, jewelers' benches, instrument workshops, and architectural studios, all unified by the shared practice of infusing memory into physical objects. The old looms still operate in the upper floors of the founding towers, but the sound that defines the Loom-Spires today is as likely to be the ring of a Smithwright's hammer on an anvil as the clack of a shuttle.

**Aesthetic:** The district sprawls across a cluster of midstream islands connected by arched stone bridges, with workshops built out over the canal on wooden jetties and stone quays. The air smells of cedar shavings, hot metal, solder flux, and the faint ozone tang of memory being worked. Storefronts display their craft openly: a jeweler's window glows with rings set with tiny Volatilis stones; a Luthwright's balcony holds drying instrument bodies suspended in memory vapor; a Smithwright's forge vents sparks that carry fleeting impressions of the Amalga being hammered into a blade.

Color and warmth distinguish the Loom-Spires from the rest of Gloamvere. Conclave artisans infuse their own buildings with ambient spiritus, not for commerce but out of habit and philosophy. Walking through the district produces a low, shifting undercurrent of feeling: warmth near a bakery whose ovens are Conclave-crafted, unease near a weapons forge where a Smithwright is working with martial Amalga, calm near an instrument shop where a Luthwright is tuning a fiddle infused with Tinctura of contentment.

**Who Lives Here:** The Bound train in ground-floor workshops under their masters, learning to sense spiritus across every craft, not just weaving. Kindles sell early work from canal-side stalls. Wrights of every specialty operate independent workshops: Threadwrights, Smithwrights, Lampwrights, Luthwrights, Masonwrights, and others. Resonants maintain larger studios, often employing several Wrights and Kindles, and adjudicate guild disputes from offices above their workshops. Athanors are rare and reclusive; their workshops are identifiable by the fact that people crossing the bridge outside *feel something*, a hush, a pull, a sense of being noticed by something inside.

**Key Landmarks:**

- **The Gossamer Bridge**: A wide thoroughfare connecting the three largest artisan islands, its railings and suspension cables woven from memory-strengthened fibers by generations of Threadwrights. The bridge resonates faintly with a sense of *belonging*, making it a natural gathering place. It is the Conclave's most visible public work and the one craft that still justifies the district's name.
- **The First Kindling Hall**: A large workshop where apprentices undergo their First Kindling, the public ceremony of completing their first unassisted infusion. The hall has absorbed decades of accumulated pride, anxiety, and relief from thousands of ceremonies. Entering it is an emotional experience whether or not you're Conclave.
- **The Fraying**: The downstream edge of the Loom-Spires where the district bleeds into poorer neighborhoods near the Dredge-Canals. This is where Shaper-aligned Wrights operate using market-bought memory of questionable provenance. The workshops here are rougher, the canal flow murkier, and the ethical debates sharper. The Tenders avoid it. The Shapers call it "the real Conclave."

**The Tender-Shaper geography:** The philosophical divide maps loosely to canal position. Tender-aligned artisans tend to work upstream, where the ambient memory is cleaner and ethically sourced material is easier to verify. Shaper-aligned artisans work downstream, closer to bulk supply chains and cheap Faded stock. This is a tendency, not a rule, but a Wright's workshop address subtly signals their ethical alignment to anyone paying attention.

#### 2. The Silent Ossuary, Old Blood Council Territory

> *"The dead do not rest. They reign."*

The Silent Ossuary occupies the oldest islands in Gloamvere, the original lagoon foundations where Arche's survivors first built after the displacement. The Ossuary is a landscape of windowless marble monoliths, sprawling cathedrals, and crypt-complexes that extend below the waterline into sealed, temperature-controlled vaults. It feels less like a neighborhood and more like an inhabited mausoleum, because that is exactly what it is.

**Aesthetic:** Cold. Heat is siphoned away by massive preservation engines to stabilize the underground vaults where ancestral Tinctura and Quintessa are stored. The temperature differential is so extreme that frost forms on exterior stonework even at the waterline. The canals running through Ossuary territory are unnaturally still, the cold slows the memory current to a near-standstill, and the flow here is darker, thicker, and quieter than anywhere else in the city. Locals say the dead are holding it back.

The only consistent sound is the rhythmic, low-frequency chanting of the Reminiscent maintaining preservation rituals, a bass drone that visitors feel in their teeth before they hear it. Light comes from Conclave-crafted memorial fixtures that burn with a warm amber glow infused with Volatilis of *remembrance*, the one Conclave product the Old Blood purchases without complaint.

**Who Lives Here:** Remnants, the unrecognized servants who maintain the physical infrastructure, occupy ground-level service quarters along the quays and are everywhere but acknowledged by no one. The Reminiscent work in the temples and ritual chambers, where Lahkriman Shroud-Bearers tend to the grieving and faithful house acolytes assist in ancestral consultations. Reliquarians control access to the vaults below the waterline, encountering one means you've reached a threshold you may not be permitted to cross. Living Reliquaries are found in the deep crypts and council chambers, communing with the ancestors they carry. Exalted Scions reside in the cathedral complexes but spend most of their time in the council chambers where the real power operates.

**Key Landmarks:**

- **The Gate of Thousand Names**: A massive obsidian archway etched with the genealogy of every noble house, standing at the main bridge entrance to the Ossuary. The carvings are memory-infused: touching a name produces a fleeting Volatilis impression of that ancestor. The gate functions as both monument and security checkpoint, the ancestral impressions in the stone react to recognized bloodlines.
- **The Lahkrima Nave**: The largest Lahkrima temple in Gloamvere. Open to all, including Dregs. This is where Shroud-Bearers accept the role of memory-bearers, consuming Tinctura of grief and trauma so the givers can continue functioning. The Nave is the reason the Old Blood retains popular legitimacy. It smells of cold stone and candle wax, and the silence inside it is not empty, it is *held*.
- **The Reliquarian's Walk**: A narrow corridor of locked vault doors connecting the major crypt-complexes below the waterline. Each door is keyed to a specific bloodline. Reliquarians patrol here in shifts. Whoever controls the Walk controls access to ancestral memory, making it the most practically important location in the Old Blood's infrastructure.

**The Faithful-Dynastic geography:** Faithful houses cluster around the temples and public-facing structures, the Lahkrima Nave, the open memorial gardens, the ritual halls along the main canals. Dynastic houses fortify their positions around the vault complexes and council chambers in the interior islands. The geography quietly reveals where each house's priorities lie: toward the people, or toward the power.

#### 3. The Glass Spine, Siderian Clade Territory

> *"To understand a thing is to honor it more deeply than reverence ever could."*

The Glass Spine takes its name from the sky-tunnels that date back to Arche's response to the Glass Rain, a catastrophe during which the Archeans connected the city through reinforced glass passageways to survive the razor-sharp atmospheric fallout. The displacement shattered most of the original engineering, but the Glass Rain itself had also left behind a lattice of fused silicate deposits across the upstream islands. The Clade claimed the surviving Archean fragments and natural formations alike, reinforced them with brass and steel, and connected them into the district's current sky-tunnel network, converting the islands into laboratories, engineering bays, archives, and administrative complexes. It is the most technologically advanced and well-lit area of Gloamvere.

**Aesthetic:** Clinical precision. White stone scrubbed clean, brass fittings polished to mirror finish, walkways lit by the cold blue-white glow of stabilized Precipia lamps. The geometry is intentional, right angles, uniform spacing, labeled pathways. After the sensory richness of the Loom-Spires and the ritual cold of the Ossuary, the Glass Spine feels *designed*. That is the point.

The canals here are channeled through engineered stone conduits rather than natural waterways. The current runs fast, bright, and carefully filtered, the Clade monitors the canal chemistry upstream of their territory and diverts contaminated flow. The air smells of ozone, copper, and stabilization compounds. The sound is mechanical: brass gears, construct joints clicking, equipment humming behind closed doors.

**Who Lives Here:** Novitiates rotate through introductory laboratories, learning the basics of all three tracks before specializing. Observers occupy the research wings, cluttered offices and laboratories that are the one part of the Glass Spine resisting the district's clinical order. Factors run the engineering bays and construct workshops where the practical work of building and maintaining Gloamvere's infrastructure happens. Adjutants operate from administrative offices near the command complexes. Logicians occupy secured facilities with classification authority. Luminaries and Arch-Logicians work from the upper levels of the central complex, connected by the glass sky-tunnels that give the district its name.

The quays and streets are scrubbed clean by Dregs pressed into maintenance labor, the Clade's meritocracy has a floor, and below it are people valued only for the upkeep they provide.

**Key Landmarks:**

- **The Archivist's Needle**: A narrow spire of glass and steel rising from the Clade's central island, housing the primary laboratory for mapping the degradation cascade. The Needle contains the faction's most sensitive instruments and most classified research. Access above the third floor requires Logician approval.
- **The Construct Yards**: Open-air engineering bays along the Clade's main quay where Factors assemble, test, and repair the automatons that patrol contested zones and maintain city infrastructure. The Yards are kept deliberately visible from surrounding canals, rows of constructs in various stages of completion, gleaming under Precipia lamps. The message is clear.
- **The Locked Archive**: A vault of classified research on the interior of the central island, accessible only by bridge from the command complex. Papers that Command has deemed sensitive are filed here. Scholars whose work ends up in the Locked Archive receive a commendation and lose control of their findings simultaneously.

**The Scholar-Engineer-Command geography:** Scholars cluster in the research wings, messy, open, collaborative spaces along the quieter canals. Engineers dominate the quayside facilities, loud, organized, practical. Command occupies the interior complex, accessible only by monitored bridges. A Novitiate rotating through all three during their first year experiences the Clade's internal culture clash as a physical journey between different islands.

### Neutral and Contested Zones

#### 4. The Grand Rib-Market, Neutral Trade Hub

> *"Everything has a price. Everything. The question is whether you're buying or being bought."*

The economic heart of Gloamvere, occupying a large midstream island at the junction where the three faction territories' canal systems converge. The Market's name comes from the exposed Aethos formations, those ancient calcified structures of unknown origin, visible in the island's lower foundations and canal walls, where builders drove pilings into the dense mineralized material for structural support. The formations are architecturally useful and visually striking, but the Market's identity is defined by trade, not geology.

**Aesthetic:** Dense, multi-leveled stalls and trading houses built along the island's quays and bridge approaches, connected by covered walkways and interior arcades. It is loud, crowded, and saturated with mixed Volatilis smoke, vendors burn samples to attract customers, creating a swirling haze of fleeting sensations: a burst of lavender, a flash of childhood warmth, the sharp tang of a forgotten argument. The canal flow around Market island is the most polluted in the city, a prismatic slick of mixed memory runoff from every faction's processing operations.

Upper stalls along the main quays sell refined product: Conclave-crafted goods, Clade-stabilized Precipia, Old Blood-authenticated heritage pieces. Interior stalls sell raw memory in bulk. The further you go from the main quays into the island's interior arcades, the cheaper and more questionable the product becomes.

**Who Works Here:** Faction rank is subordinated to economic function. A Conclave Wright selling crafted instruments operates from the same quay as a Clade Factor offering stabilization services and an Old Blood Reminiscent authenticating heritage Tinctura. The Market has its own informal hierarchy, veteran stallholders, guild factors who broker bulk deals, and the mixed-faction patrol that keeps the peace.

**Key Landmarks:**

- **The Bone Nave**: The largest trading floor, built in a natural cavern within the island where the Aethos formations create a vaulted ceiling. High-value auctions occur here, rare Tinctura, specialist Precipia, Conclave masterworks. The acoustics amplify the auctioneer's voice into a resonant boom.
- **The Assayer's Row**: A line of independent assessors along the main quay who evaluate memory quality, authenticity, and degradation state. They are the closest thing the Market has to neutral authority.
- **The Leach Line**: The unofficial border where legitimate trade ends and black-market activity begins, marked by the point where the quayside stalls give way to canal-boat vendors and improvised jetty markets extending toward the Dredge-Canals downstream. Below the Leach Line, you can buy Distorted product at a discount, sell memories of dubious extraction, and hire people who don't ask questions.

**Patrol:** Maintained by a joint force drawn from all three factions, Conclave Wrights, Old Blood Reminiscent, and Clade Factors operating under a rotating command structure. The patrol keeps the peace; it does not enforce justice. Those are different things.

#### 5. The Dredge-Canals, Lawless Slums

> *"Down here, even your feelings are secondhand."*

The furthest downstream reach of Gloamvere's canal system, where the heavy Leached runoff from every refinery, workshop, and processing operation in the city finally settles. The Dredge-Canals are not a district anyone designed, they are what accumulated at the bottom of the current. The Dregs, the desperate, and the black-market operators who work outside every faction's structures have built a community in the sediment.

**Aesthetic:** A fog-choked labyrinth of rotting wooden jetties, rusted iron walkways, and improvised structures built on and between whatever the canal bed offered, old pilings, scuttled barges, and in the deepest reaches, exposed Aethos formations that the builders above considered too deep to be worth excavating. The "water" here is barely liquid, a thick, grey, barely luminous sludge that occasionally sparks with a Faded memory fragment: a flash of color, a whisper of music, a feeling of something lost. The fog is dense because low-grade memory vapor has nowhere to go, it pools in the downstream hollows and carries uninvited feelings stronger and stranger than the Canal Drift above.

Sound carries wrong here. A conversation at normal volume travels three bridges; a shout gets swallowed entirely. Navigation is by landmark and instinct, there are no street signs, no Precipia lamps, no Conclave-crafted fixtures. The only light comes from burning Leached dregs in crude braziers, which gives the district a dim, shifting, grey-violet glow.

**Who Lives Here:** Dregs, the calcined who have lost their Quintessa, wander in varying states of autonomic function, cared for by no one except the occasional Lahkriman cleric who descends from the Ossuary. Desperate people who have sold their memories down to their last Solvate survive on labor, theft, or selling what little remains. Black-market Shapers, artisans who left or were expelled from the Conclave, operate workshops using cheap Faded and Distorted stock the official guilds won't touch.

The Dredge-Canals are the primary recruitment ground for the **Hollow Flame.** The heretics find their most receptive audience among people who have watched the Old Blood weep for the tragedy of Calcination while doing nothing about the economic conditions that cause it.

**Key Landmarks:**

- **The Grey Market**: A semi-permanent market on a cluster of lashed-together barges that trades exclusively in Leached and Faded memory. Everything sold here is bottom-tier, grey, contextless feelings stripped of their names. This is where memory goes to die, and where the poorest people in Gloamvere buy the only warmth they can afford.
- **The Still**: A hidden distillery where black-market operators attempt to refine Leached dregs back into usable Volatilis. The process is crude, the results unreliable, and the side effects for the refiner, chronic Drift, emotional numbness, nosebleeds, are occupational hazards. The Still moves regularly to avoid Clade patrols.
- **The Dregs Walk**: Not a landmark but a phenomenon. At certain hours, groups of Dregs move through the Canals in aimless, synchronized patterns no one can explain. The Conclave believes the calx is responding to ambient spiritus. The Clade believes it is a neurological artifact. The Old Blood believes the ancestors are trying to call them home.

#### 6. The Unmoored Commons, Cross-Faction Neutral Ground

> *"The only place in Gloamvere where your rank means less than what you're saying."*

A cluster of taverns, coffeehouses, debate halls, and informal meeting spaces that has grown organically along the bridge-heads and quays where the three faction territories' canal routes intersect. The Unmoored Commons is not a planned district, it is what happens when people from different factions need a place to meet, argue, drink, and conspire without faction walls listening.

**Aesthetic:** Eclectic. A Conclave-crafted fixture hangs over a Clade-designed table in a building whose foundations were blessed by an Old Blood priest. The architecture is hybrid and unplanned, a patchwork of styles that reflects cross-faction traffic. The spaces tend to be warm, crowded, and loud, because neutrality requires witnesses and anonymity requires noise. The canals here carry the mixed currents of all three faction territories, making the flow an unpredictable swirl of color, sometimes bright, sometimes dark, occasionally sparking with stray fragments from upstream.

**Who Comes Here:** Cross-faction sympathizers are the Commons' lifeblood. A Clade Observer and a Conclave Tender discussing shared concerns about memory degradation. An Old Blood Reminiscent and a Scholar exchanging research over drinks. A Hollow Flame sympathizer passing a pamphlet to a young Reliquarian who has started asking uncomfortable questions.

The Commons is also where fixers, freelancers, and unaffiliated operators find work. Player characters will likely spend significant time here, it is the natural hub for quest hooks, faction contacts, and NPC encounters that cross political lines.

**Key Landmarks:**

- **The Residuum**: The most well-known tavern in the Commons, named for the alchemical term for "what remains after the process is complete." It serves drinks infused with low-arousal Volatilis, a legal and mildly addictive practice that gives each beverage a faint emotional undertone. Default meeting place for cross-faction business. Its owner, whose faction affiliation is a matter of active speculation, enforces one rule: no extraction on the premises.
- **The Paradox Room**: A debate hall on a converted barge, maintained by Clade Scholars who believe open discourse matters more than classified research. Hosts cross-faction academic exchanges, philosophical arguments, and occasionally shouting matches. Named for the observation that the Clade's most open-minded members leave Clade territory to practice the Clade's founding principle.

#### 7. The Char, Contested Ruins / Hollow Flame Territory

> *"If you won't share the fire, and you won't let anyone else make fire, are you really protecting the sacred, or are you just keeping everyone in the dark?"*

The Char is the burned-out husk of a neighborhood destroyed in a fire of disputed origin two generations ago. The fire, which the Old Blood calls arson, the Clade calls an industrial accident, and the Hollow Flame calls a "necessary illumination", gutted a section of canal-side buildings between the Ossuary and the Dredge-Canals. The ruins were never rebuilt. All three factions claim jurisdiction. None invest resources. The Char has become de facto territory for the Hollow Flame and others who exist outside formal power structures.

**Aesthetic:** Scorched stone quays, collapsed canal-side buildings with their interiors exposed to the current, charred bridge stumps leading nowhere. The ruins are not entirely dead, squatters have built shelters in the more intact structures, and the Hollow Flame has established a network of safehouses behind fire-blackened walls. The air carries the faint acrid scent of old ash mixed with the ozone of unstabilized memory, the fire released stored spiritus from the destroyed buildings, and the residue never fully dissipated. At night, patches of the ruins glow faintly with prismatic light as trapped memory fragments cycle through degradation. The canal current here is strangely warm, heated by whatever is still burning, slowly, in the foundations.

**Who Lives Here:** Hollow Flame adherents, from nonviolent reformers to radical redistributionists, use the Char as their base. Other residents include people who have fallen through every safety net: evicted Remnants, disgraced Wrights, burned-out Clade researchers suffering from Drift, and anyone who needs to disappear.

**Key Landmarks:**

- **The Ember Nave**: A ruined chapel, formerly Old Blood, repurposed as a Hollow Flame gathering hall. The fire-scarred walls have been painted with the movement's iconography, a flame with no fuel, burning inside an empty lantern. This is where the moderate and radical wings argue about method, and the most likely location for player characters to make first contact with the Hollow Flame.
- **The Break**: A gap in the ruins where a collapsed building exposed a previously sealed Old Blood vault. The vault was empty when found, or so the official story goes. The Hollow Flame claims it contained ancestral Tinctura that the dynastic houses recovered before anyone could verify the contents. Whether this is true is an open question. The Break has become a symbol for the Hollow Flame's core argument: the Old Blood hoards memory behind locked doors and calls it sacred, and when those doors break open, nothing is there because they moved it all before anyone could see.

### Beyond the City Walls, Kavath

Gloamvere is the largest settlement in known Kavath, but it is not the only one. Beyond the outermost districts, the demi-plane extends outward as a memory-saturated landscape with three distinct strata.

*(Full cosmological detail: see Part II, Cosmology & Metaphysics.)*

#### The Memory Wilderness (Surface)

The terrain beyond the city walls is real but shaped by five centuries of uncontained memory accumulating with no one to harvest it. Near the city: thin wilderness with Volatilis haze and fine crystalline residue. Further out: memory density increases, creating terrain features shaped by emotional content, grief valleys, rage ridges, joy hollows. The Clade's survey expeditions have mapped perhaps ten percent of the accessible surface.

Memory-warped fauna inhabit the wilderness, creatures formed from concentrated memory the way crystals form from saturated solution. Daggerheart adversaries can be reskinned as memory constructs and instinct-formations.

Wild memory deposits in the wilderness are economically valuable. Expeditions to harvest wild memory are lucrative and dangerous.

#### The Fragments (Displaced Communities)

Scattered across the memory wilderness are the Fragments, pieces of Arche pulled into Kavath by the dimensional tear's residual effects. Over the centuries, villages, stretches of farmland, and occasionally solitary buildings have materialized. Some arrived centuries ago and built their own cultures around memory. Some are recent arrivals, disoriented and resource-poor. Some didn't survive, dead Fragments are ghost towns where failed inhabitants' memories still linger.

Trade routes between Gloamvere and the larger established Fragment settlements exist, maintained by bargemen and couriers who've learned to read the emotional terrain.

#### The Aethos Substrate (Deep)

Beneath everything, the Aethos bone formations extend throughout Kavath as a vast subterranean structure. Memory behaves differently underground, it flows through bone corridors like water through aqueducts, suggesting the Aethos designed their architecture to manage cognitive substance long before humans arrived. Gloamvere sits on one of the densest substrate nodes.

#### The Settled Region

In the Modern Era (~400–500+ post-displacement), Gloamvere is the cultural and economic capital of a settled region that includes established towns, frontier outposts, and Fragment communities. Some outer settlements are loyal partners, some resentful tributaries, some fully independent. The factions extend influence outward, Conclave guild halls in allied towns, Clade research stations at distant substrate sites, Old Blood vault-chapels, but their grip weakens with distance.

### How Borders Work in Gloamvere

In a canal city defined by cognitive substance, borders are enforced by the nature of memory itself as much as by bridges and patrols. Each faction has developed border security that reflects its philosophy, and its craft.

#### Emotional Barriers, Spiritus Conclave

The Conclave uses **Woven Wards**: banners, architectural elements, and bridge-mounted installations infused with high-intensity Tinctura of *unwelcome*, *dread*, or *disorientation*. These wards radiate their emotional payload to anyone not carrying a resonant frequency key, a small Conclave-crafted token that neutralizes the effect on the bearer.

The wards are effective but imprecise. They don't distinguish between a genuine intruder and a lost traveler. A player character crossing a bridge into Conclave territory without a key will feel a mounting sense that they should not be here, anxiety, wrongness, the conviction that they've made a terrible mistake, long before they encounter any physical barrier. The sensation fades immediately upon leaving range.

Wards are crafted by Wrights and maintained by Resonants. Quality reflects skill: a poorly made ward leaks emotional bleed into surrounding canal fog, while a masterwork ward has a precise boundary that switches on and off like a door.

#### Algorithmic Patrols, Siderian Clade

The Clade uses **Vigilance Constructs**: automatons loaded with Amalga of experienced soldiers and programmed with behavioral recognition patterns. These constructs patrol the bridges and quays at the Glass Spine's perimeter, identifying unauthorized individuals by analyzing gait, movement patterns, and psychic signature irregularities.

The constructs are efficient but inflexible. They follow programmed parameters. A person who walks with the confidence of someone who belongs may pass unnoticed. A person who hesitates or radiates stress will be stopped. The constructs do not exercise judgment, they execute criteria. This means they are simultaneously easy to fool (for someone who knows the criteria) and impossible to reason with (for someone who doesn't).

Vigilance Constructs are built by Factors and programmed by Logicians. The Amalga loaded into them is sourced from retired soldiers, a practice the Conclave considers trapping living spiritus and the Old Blood considers desecration.

#### The Law of Blood, Old Blood Council

In Old Blood territory, your entry visa is a lineage check. The Ossuary's walls, bridges, and canal-side structures are infused with ancestral Tinctura, preserved impressions of Old Blood family lines going back centuries. These impressions respond to recognized bloodlines by remaining stable and navigable. For unrecognized visitors, the district becomes a navigational nightmare: bridges that were crossable now seem to lead in circles, doors that were open are now closed, and the chanting of the Reminiscent seems to come from every direction.

This is not deliberate cruelty, it is a side effect of the preservation architecture. The ancestral memory in the walls maintains coherence for recognized lineages and treats everything else as noise. The result is the same: if the dead don't know you, you're not getting in without an escort.

Non-bloodline visitors must be accompanied by a Reliquarian or higher, whose presence signals the ancestral memory to accept the outsider. This makes Reliquarians the literal gatekeepers of Old Blood territory, a fact that gives their rank practical power far beyond its formal description.

#### Liminal Zones, The Unmoored Paths

The bridges and canal-ways between districts are known as **Unmoored Paths**: transitional spaces where no single faction's influence fully holds. Many of these paths cross the canals at points where the Veil (Kavath's boundary with what lies beyond) is thinner than normal.

Travelers on Unmoored Paths keep memory vials corked tightly to prevent **Environmental Drift**: the tendency for carried memories to resonate with ambient instability and degrade faster than normal. Experienced travelers also report subjective time distortion on longer crossings: a bridge that should take two minutes might feel like ten, depending on the Veil's current state.

The Unmoored Paths are maintained by no one and used by everyone. They are the arteries of daily life, and their unpredictable nature is one of the many things Gloamvereis have learned to accept without understanding.

### Design Notes

#### Structural Decisions

**Why a canal city:** Gloamvere's economy runs on liquid memory, the canals literalize this. Canal flow direction creates a natural gradient of quality and wealth without needing a vertical hierarchy. Bridges are chokepoints, which gives border mechanics physical reality. Water carries sound, fog, and feeling, which makes the city sensorially rich for narrative description.

**Why 7 districts:** Three faction territories, two distinct neutral zones (trade vs. social), one lawless zone, one contested wildcard. Every district serves a different narrative function with no redundancy.

**Why light rank mapping:** Noting where ranks *tend* to concentrate while allowing exceptions preserves the messiness of a real city. A Resonant might choose to work in the Fraying. A Remnant might serve upstream because the Exalted Scion they attend requires it. Tendencies, not rules.

**Conclave aesthetic diversity:** The Loom-Spires name is historical, not descriptive. The district must read as a full artisan guild, forges, carpentry shops, jewelers, instrument workshops, architectural studios, not a weaver's commune.
