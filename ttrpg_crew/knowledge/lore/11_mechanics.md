### The Dreg Condition (3-Stage Mechanic)

#### Overview

Dreg status is not an ancestry or community, it is a **condition** that can occur during play when a character's core identity is compromised. It represents the in-game consequence of Calcination or severe memory loss.

#### Trigger

The GM may impose the Dreg Condition when:
- A character's Quintessa (Tier 6 memory) is forcibly extracted
- A character suffers a catastrophic memory-related event (GM discretion)
- A character voluntarily undergoes a process that strips core identity

#### Mechanical Effects

**Stage 1, Fraying:** The character's sense of self is damaged but present. They have disadvantage on rolls involving personal motivation, memory recall, or resisting identity manipulation. They occasionally lose focus mid-action. This stage is recoverable through rest, Conclave therapeutic intervention, or narrative resolution.

**Stage 2, Eroding:** The character's identity is severely compromised. In addition to Stage 1 effects, they lose access to one community feature (GM's choice or player's choice) and cannot spend Hope on behalf of other characters. They may have difficulty recognizing allies or remembering their own goals. Recovery requires significant intervention, extended downtime, specialized treatment, or a quest-level narrative arc.

**Stage 3, Calcined (Full Dreg):** The character's Quintessa is gone. They become an NPC. This is effectively character death, the body lives, but the person is absent. This stage should only occur as a dramatic narrative consequence, never as a random mechanical punishment. Recovery from Stage 3 is unprecedented, Clara Moree is the only known case, and would constitute a major campaign arc if attempted.

#### Design Notes

The Dreg Condition exists to make Calcination mechanically real without making it a constant threat. Stage 1 should come up occasionally in Gloamvere campaigns as a consequence of dangerous memory work or faction conflict. Stage 2 should be rare and dramatic. Stage 3 should be a once-per-campaign event at most, the nuclear option that raises the stakes for everyone at the table.

The condition also creates mechanical space for Dregborne PCs, a character with the Holdfast community feature has advantage on resisting the very effects that lead to Stage 1, making their background directly relevant to gameplay.

#### Quick Reference

| Stage | Name | Key Effect | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Fraying | Disadvantage on identity/motivation rolls | Rest, Conclave therapy, narrative |
| **2** | Eroding | Lose 1 community feature, cannot spend Hope for others | Extended downtime, specialized treatment, quest arc |
| **3** | Calcined | Character becomes NPC (effective death) | Unprecedented, major campaign arc |

### Existing Mechanics (Pending Formal Write-Up)

The following mechanics exist conceptually in the lore and need formal Daggerheart-language treatment:
- **Stress Bargain** - If a character is forced to mark Stress but their Stress track is completely full, the character may opt to either mark a Hit Point (as normal) or they can clear their Stress track immediately but accept **Stage 1** of the **Dreg Condition**.

- **Cerebral Overload**: Consuming a memory more than 2 tiers above your level requires a Presence or Intellect action roll (GM's discretion based on the memory's nature) to integrate the spiritus. Failure results in marking a Stress.
 - **Difficulty**: Set by the GM, usually scaling with the memory's Tier.
 - **Success with Hope**: You process the memory perfectly. You gain its narrative or mechanical benefit and clear 1 Stress.
 - **Success with Fear**: You gain the benefits, but the cognitive weight is heavy. Mark 1 Stress.
 - **Failure with Hope**: You fail to extract the benefit, but you manage to safely vent the excess spiritus before it damages you.
 - **Failure with Fear (Cerebral Overload)**: The memory overloads your nervous system. You mark 2 Stress (if your Stress track is full, mark a Hit Point instead) and take the **Vulnerable** condition until you take a Short Rest or an ally uses an action to stabilize you.
- **Overwrite**: Consuming a Tier 6 memory (Quintessa) is incredibly dangerous because it contains the foundation of a person's identity. Contest of wills where the consumed personality may take control of the host body.

 **Mechanic: Contest of Wills**
 Attempting to consume a Quintessa triggers an immediate **Overwrite Contest**. The character must make a **Presence** action roll against a Very Hard or Severe difficulty threshold (e.g., 20+).
 - **Success**: The character dominates the Quintessa. They retain their identity, gain profound knowledge from the consumed life, and gain 3 Hope.
 - **Failure**: The Quintessa asserts dominance over the host. The character instantly suffers the **Eroding** stage of the **Dreg Condition**. Additionally, the GM may dictate that the consumed personality takes control of the character's body for the remainder of the scene, or require the player to roleplay the foreign identity's goals until they receive specialized Conclave therapy.

**Canal Drift and District effects**
 - **The Dredge-Canals (Hazard)**: The ambient emotional fog is thick and oppressive. When taking a Short Rest here, clearing Stress costs 1 additional Hope. Additionally, the GM can spend 1 Fear at any time to force a character to experience a localized hallucination from the Drift, requiring an **Instinct** roll to avoid marking 1 Stress.
 - **Lahkrima Nave (Sanctuary)**: The crystalline resonance of the Conclave's district stabilizes spiritus. Characters who take a Short Rest here automatically clear 1 additional Stress or gain 1 Hope for free.

 **Mechanic: Perks and Debts**
When a character formally aligns with a Faction (The Conclave, The Siderian Clade, or The Old Blood), they gain the following:
- **Faction Perk (Player Tool)**: Once per session, you may spend 2 Hope to automatically gain Advantage on any roll that leverages your faction's resources, jurisdiction, or specific expertise.
- **Faction Debt (GM Tool)**: The GM gains a specific way to spend Fear against you. For example, the GM can spend 2 Fear to impose a Faction Mandate; if you act against your faction's explicit interests during the current scene, you roll with Disadvantage.

 **Mechanic: Inhaling Tinctura**
As an action, a character can consume a stabilized Tier 1 memory (Tinctura).
- **The Boon**: The character may immediately clear 1 Stress, OR they may claim Advantage on their next action roll if they can narratively justify how the specific emotion/skill in the Tinctura helps them.
- **The Catch**: Because consuming memory in the heat of the moment is jarring, the character must roll a Duality Die. If Fear is the higher result, the GM gains 1 Fear as the memory temporarily distracts or disorients them.
