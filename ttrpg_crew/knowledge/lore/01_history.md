### Core Terms (Quick Reference)

- **Gloamvere:** The city/settlement, before and after displacement. (Not the plane.)
- **Arche:** The original Material Plane; Gloamvere City’s world of origin.
- **Kavath:** The demi-plane Gloamvere City occupies post-displacement; the broader “world/region” of modern play.
- **The Veil:** The membrane-like boundary enclosing Kavath; semi-permeable, intention-reactive, thin at thresholds.
- **Thin Spots:** Structured weaknesses in the Veil at liminal spaces (doorframes, archways, open graves, empty rooms).
- **Tinctura / Quintessa:** Preserved memory artifacts (essence vs deep identity/life-experience tier).
- **Canal Drift:** Refinement runoff (vapor/emotional residue); usually contextless, sometimes anomalous downstream.
- **Spiritus:** Conclave framing of memory as living essence with agency-like behavior.
- **Substrate:** Altered foundational material beneath/around the city; transformed by Veil exposure and Aethos resonance.
- **Volatilis → Athanor:** Seven-tier standardized memory currency hierarchy (alchemical naming v3.0).

---

Gloamvere City has existed in Kavath for over five hundred years.

No one alive remembers Arche. More importantly: no living person has ever met a living Archean. What remains are inherited echoes, Tinctura sealed in Old Blood vaults, oral traditions, and Living Reliquaries who absorb the dead’s memories and report what they find.

For the vast majority of Gloamvere City’s population, Arche is scripture, not history. The sun is a theological concept. The ocean is a word that means “the thing the Veil replaced.”

This distance is essential to understanding modern politics. The Old Blood’s authority rests on possessing the only first-hand records of a world no one else can verify. The Clade’s skepticism toward Old Blood theology is partly rooted in the fact that the Old Blood’s “evidence” is five centuries of sealed vaults that no outsider is permitted to audit. The Conclave’s animist philosophy developed entirely within Kavath, they have no lived memory of Arche’s sun and do not miss it the way the Old Blood insists everyone should.

**GM Use (Overview)**
- Treat “truth” as *gated*: sealed, priced, rationed, classified.
- Keep nouns clean at the table: **Gloamvere City** (place), **Kavath** (plane/region), **Veil** (boundary).
- When you need instant conflict: introduce a memory artifact that threatens who controls the origin story.

### Gloamvere City in Arche: The Jewel of the Tides

Before the displacement, Gloamvere was not merely a city; it was the bottleneck of the world’s economy. Situated on a cluster of lagoon islands at the mouth of the Vastus River in the Material Plane of **Arche**, it was a Venice-like marvel of white stone, deep canals, and towering merchant spires. It was said that gold did not flow in Gloamvere, it settled like silt.

The Gloamverei (also called Archeans, referencing their world of origin) were a people forged in fire and salt. Not soft merchants, but hardened survivors who viewed commerce as a form of warfare and survival.

#### Arche, The Wider World

Arche is a full Material Plane with continents, nations, diverse peoples, geography, and history beyond Gloamvere City. The city was the economic bottleneck, not the entirety of the world. The region surrounding the city gives every Daggerheart ancestry a plausible point of origin:

**Upriver, The Vastus Hinterlands.** Agricultural plains, mountain passes, and mining communities along the Vastus River and its tributaries. Raw materials flowed downriver to Gloamvere for processing and trade. Dwarves, humans, and orcs had deep roots here, mining settlements, farming communities, and river-trade towns that fed the city’s economy.

**Coastal Regions.** Fishing communities, port towns, and naval traditions along the coastline surrounding Gloamvere’s lagoon. Galapa, ribbets, and seaborne cultures thrived here. The Silver Bellies calamity affected the entire coast, not just the city.

**Forested Interior.** Deep old-growth wilderness away from the river trade routes. Firbolg, faun, elf, and fungril territory. These communities traded with Gloamvere but were not dependent on it, they had their own economies and cultures.

**The Aethos Ranges.** Mountain or highland region where the giant-kin Aethos originally lived before their civilization fell. Giants still inhabit these areas in the present day. The burial mounds that became Gloamvere’s foundation were an extension of this territory, the Aethos buried their dead on the coastal islands the way some cultures use offshore sacred sites.

**Other Nations.** Gloamvere was a trade hub, not an empire. Multiple sovereign states, kingdoms, and independent communities sent merchants through the city. Drakona clans, infernis enclaves, katari nomads, simiah arboreal cities, faerie courts, all existed in Arche with varying relationships to Gloamvere’s economy.

#### The Foundation: The Ossuary Isles

The original settlers of Gloamvere were exiles looking for a defensible harbor on Arche. They found the lagoon islands, perfect for deep-draft ships, but ignored the warnings of local druidic circles. The islands were not natural formations. They were the Cairn-Crown: a ring of burial isles raised over the dead of the Grisag, an ancient giant-kin people who predated the common era.

In their last age, the Grisag attempted to stitch shut a planar faultline beneath the Cairn-Crown. They raised a ring of cairns and laced the island-bedrock with minerite and marrow-runes, building a lattice meant to give the tear weight, to pin the faultline in place the way a keel pins a ship against tide. Then, in a single gathered rite, they bound their dead and their living memories into that lattice, anchoring it with willing sacrifice: names surrendered, lives distilled, whole lineages poured into the seal.

It held for a heartbeat. Then the faultline inverted, not sealed, but answered, and what poured through was the Unremembering, an unmaking that stripped away matter, memory, and meaning. The force of it calcined the Grisag where they stood, fusing them into one colossal reef-mass of bone: the Cairn-Crown’s true foundation.

Gloamvere was built literally atop those remains. The foundations of the Grand Market were driven into titan-bone, and the cellars of the noble houses were carved deep into the pale, porous mass. That desecration provided a potent, thrumming current of sorcerous industry, but it also cursed the land to perpetual turbulence. Those who lived “in the bones” complained of odd dreams: waking in cold sweats from nightmares that wouldn’t hold shape, glimpses of the Unremembering, or a sliver of the Grisag’s last cries as they were rent apart.

Over time, the city learned to monetize what it could not escape. In Gloamvere, remembrance is a resource and your past buys your future, whether you want it to or not.

When the Rite of Unmaking tore Gloamvere from Arche, it did not leave its foundations behind. The city arrived in Kavath with the Cairn-Crown still welded beneath it, and the Veil began its slow work.

These Grisag remains survive in modern Gloamvere, though five centuries of Veil exposure have transformed them, the minerite threaded through the Cairn-Crown has partially crystallized into a cognitive substrate, harder, stranger, and more resonant than the material the Archeans built on.

In places where the canals run deepest, particularly in the downstream reaches and beneath the Marrow Market, excavations have exposed massive curved formations that resemble ribs or vertebrae embedded in the bedrock. The Clade classifies these as geological formations from the pre-displacement substrate. The Old Blood calls them the bones of the Grisag and considers them sacred. The Conclave says they hum faintly when touched, which suggests residual spiritus, and the humming has grown louder over the centuries, which suggests the Veil is still changing them.

#### The Era of Calamities, The Forging

Gloamvere was never safe. The spirits of the Aethos and the disturbed ley lines lashed out repeatedly. Yet with every disaster, the Archeans refused to move. Instead, they rebuilt stronger, developing a cultural identity of obstinate defiance.

**1. The Summer of Silver Bellies**

In the city’s second century, the ocean recoiled. For three months, the harbor did not fill with water but with the carcasses of deep-sea life. Beached whales, dead krakens, and millions of silver-scaled fish rotted in the heat. The stench was lethal, carrying a plague that turned skin to grey scale.

*The Archean Response:* They invented advanced filtration masks and alchemical preservatives. They harvested the oil and bones of the dead leviathans, turning a plague into the city’s most profitable export year for lamp oil and scrimshaw. The filtration masks were later adapted for memory vapor after the displacement, the ancestors of the Canal Drift Masks still used in Gloamvere City today.

**2. The Pyre of Stone**

Forty years later, a magical resonance caused the granite of the city’s famous Sea Wall to ignite. It did not melt; it burned with cold, purple flames that could not be doused by water. The fires raged for weeks, threatening to consume the residential districts.

*The Archean Response:* The Mason’s Guild developed “Null-Mortar,” a binding agent made from crushed abjuration crystals. They rebuilt the wall while it was still smoldering. The citizens adopted fire-resistant silk clothing, which became a high-fashion trend across the continent.

The cold purple fire, notably the same color as the Veil’s sky, is a detail the Old Blood’s theologians have never satisfactorily explained. Was the Pyre a natural resonance, or the first sign that the Aethos crypts connected to something beyond the Material Plane?

**3. The Year of the Glass Rain**

A localized atmospheric anomaly caused the rain over Arche to freeze into razor-sharp shards of ice and solidified mana. To walk outside was to be flayed.

*The Archean Response:* They connected the city entirely through reinforced glass sky-tunnels and expanded the crypt-tunnels beneath the city. Trade never stopped; it just moved underground and overhead. The sky-tunnels were the engineering ancestors of the Clade’s modern pneumatic tube system, the same instinct (enclose, pressurize, keep moving) applied to different problems.

By the end of this era, the people of Arche were considered the most steadfast in the world. Their motto was *"Adversa non frangunt"*, Adversity does not break us.

**GM Use (Arche Era)**
- Use calamities as cultural DNA: endure, engineer, monetize survival.
- Seed artifacts from each calamity as plot catalysts (mask tech, Null-Mortar, tunnel schematics).
- If you want foreshadowing without answers, make the Pyre’s color recur as a “Veil signature.”

### The Cult of the Fractured Mirror

As Gloamvere City reached its zenith, an insidious rot took hold in the shadows. The Cult of the Fractured Mirror, worshipers of a Mistress of Discord known as Lady Nyxentia, infiltrated the city. They did not seek wealth; they despised the Archean order and the rigid structure of the trade guilds.

The Cult believed that Gloamvere’s resilience was an affront to the natural chaos of the universe. They sought to shatter the city’s spirit by breaking the barrier between the material world and the chaotic energies beneath the Aethos burial grounds.

#### Who Was Lady Nyxentia?

The historical record, what survived the displacement in the memories of the first generation, offers contradictory accounts. Some Tinctura preserved in Old Blood vaults describe Nyxentia as a mortal woman: a disgraced scholar of planar theory who discovered that the Aethos crypts sat on a convergence of dimensional boundaries. Others describe her as something older, an entity that predated the cult, whispering through the crypt walls for centuries, that the cultists did not summon but *answered*.

A smaller body of testimony (fragmentary, contested, often dismissed as Drift-tainted) suggests a third possibility: “Nyxentia” was neither a single mortal nor a single entity, but a name the cult used for whatever spoke through the burial grounds, mask, title, and invitation all at once.

The distinction matters because it determines what the Veil *is*.

If Nyxentia was mortal, the Rite was a catastrophic accident, a planar tear caused by amateurs meddling with forces they didn’t understand. The Veil is a natural demi-plane that the Rite happened to breach, and Gloamvere City is trapped in it by physics, not malice.

If Nyxentia was something else, something that wanted Arche displaced, that wanted a city full of people torn from reality and deposited in a pocket dimension where their lived experience would slowly become tangible substance, then the Veil is not a prison. It is a *harvest*.

The Old Blood’s official position: Nyxentia was a mortal heretic and the Rite was a tragedy. The Veil is a prison. Gloamvere will endure until deliverance comes.

The Clade’s official position: Nyxentia’s nature is irrelevant. The Rite created measurable planar displacement. The Veil has measurable properties. Understanding those properties is more productive than arguing about the intentions of a cult figure preserved only in contradictory records.

The Conclave has no official position, but their oldest oral traditions, passed down through five centuries of master-apprentice lineage, include a detail the other factions don’t discuss: that the spiritus within Gloamvere City’s memory sometimes behaves as though it is being *watched*. Not consumed. Not manipulated. Watched. With patience.

No faction has resolved the question. It remains open, and it is one of the most dangerous questions in Gloamvere City, because the answer determines whether escape is possible, whether the economy is natural or engineered, and whether the Veil has an owner.

**GM Use (Cult Section)**
- Nyxentia is a dial: accident (political tragedy) ↔ harvest (cosmic horror) ↔ “name-as-interface” (weird-theology).
- Always attribute certainty to *someone* (a faction, a vault record, a whispered tradition), not the narrator.
- Keep “the watching” quiet: rare, deniable, and socially costly to speak aloud.

### The Rite of Unmaking

**Naming clarity:** *The Rite of Dissonance* is the cult’s ritual name. *The Rite of Unmaking* is what history calls the event.

On the night of the High Tide Festival, while the city celebrated with mask and firework, the cultists gathered in the deepest crypt, the Marrow Vault. They performed the Rite of Dissonance, sacrificing the harmony of the city to fuel a tear in reality.

They intended to topple the towers and flood the streets. They succeeded, but the result was far worse than simple destruction.

The Rite interacted with the necrotic energy of the ancient burial grounds and the stubborn will of the Archean people. The city refused to be destroyed, and reality refused to let it stay.

A violet shockwave, silent and terrifying, swept outward. The sea vanished. The sun was snuffed out.

#### What Happened to the Cult

The cultists died performing the Rite. All of them. The Marrow Vault, the deepest crypt, the convergence point, was ground zero for the planar displacement. The Rite did not merely tear reality; it *inverted* it at the point of casting. The cultists were not displaced with the city. They were dissolved, their physical bodies, their memories, their identities unmade at the moment of maximum contact with the dimensional boundary.

This is the first and oldest irony of Gloamvere City: the people who destroyed Arche did not survive to see what they had created. They got exactly what they asked for, the shattering of order, the dissolution of structure, and it consumed them first. They are not martyrs. They are not villains who escaped justice. They are *nothing*. Not even Dregs. The Rite did not calcine them; it annihilated them.

No Tinctura of the cultists’ final moments exists. No Quintessa was left to preserve. The Marrow Vault is a void in Gloamvere City’s historical record, the one event for which there are no first-hand memory artifacts, because everyone present was erased.

The Old Blood considers this divine justice. The Clade considers it a data gap. The Conclave finds it suspicious, every other event in Gloamvere City’s history left memory residue somewhere. The Rite left none. Either the cultists were truly annihilated, or whatever happened to them is being hidden by the same force that keeps the Veil intact.

#### What Lady Nyxentia Got (Speculative)

If the Cult was destroyed, and if “Nyxentia” was merely a mortal heretic, some argue the story ends here, a tragedy with no villain, only consequences.

But the Veil exists. And the Veil has properties that no neutral boundary should have.

**Clarifying the boundary (rules consistency):** 
A person who walks into the Veil itself does not come back. That is the raw boundary, unstructured, lethal, unkeyed. Thin Spots are different. They are structured weaknesses that behave like doors: passages shaped by thresholds and intention. You do not “enter the Veil” through a Thin Spot. You pass through a fault in it.

The Veil weakens at portals, archways, and places of passage, thresholds. It responds to *intention*. A doorframe with no door, an open grave, an empty room, the same Thin Spots used for Preservation rituals are also the points where the Veil is thinnest. The Old Blood says this is because thresholds are liminal and liminal spaces resonate with planar mechanics. The Clade agrees with the physics. Neither explains *why* the Veil would be thin at the precise points where Gloamvere City’s memory economy most depends on it being thin.

The sky whispers. The Old Blood says these are the regrets of the living, reflecting off the Veil’s inner surface. The Clade says the whispers are sub-threshold auditory hallucinations caused by ambient memory vapor interacting with the cochlear nerve. Neither explanation accounts for the occasional reports, rare, unverified, dismissed by all three factions officially, of the whispers forming *words*. Not Archean words. Not any language spoken in Gloamvere City.

The canals carry feelings that don’t match anyone’s memories. Canal Drift is explained as memory vapor, the gaseous waste of refinement, carrying contextless emotion. Most Canal Drift is exactly that. But downstream, in the Dredge-Canals where the sludge is thickest and the filtration weakest, people occasionally report feelings that are *alien*, emotions that don’t map to human experience. Not grief, not joy, not fear. Something else. Something that feels like being *catalogued*.

None of this is proof of anything. All of it has mundane explanations. But taken together, it creates a pattern that looks like this: the Cult died, and whatever they served got what it wanted. Not a destroyed city. A displaced one. A city where human experience slowly becomes extractable substance. A city wrapped in a membrane that responds to intention and whispers in no known language.

If Nyxentia is the Veil, if the Veil is not a prison but a *body*, and Gloamvere City exists inside it, then the entire memory economy is not a natural adaptation to displacement. It is a feeding mechanism. And the people of Gloamvere City have spent five centuries perfecting it.

*(GM guidance: The Veil and Nyxentia are deliberately ambiguous narrative dials, see the GM Use blocks throughout this chapter.)*

**GM Use (Rite Section)**
- The Marrow Vault “no residue” void is an evergreen engine for delves, research, and taboo.
- Make the Veil consistent in behavior but unclear in intention: reliable phenomena, disputed meaning.
- “Catalogued” is a strong recurring downstream vibe, use it sparingly so it stays uncanny.

### The Dark Years (~0–40 Post-Displacement)

#### The Immediate Aftermath

Gloamvere City was ripped from the Material Plane of Arche entirely. It was deposited into **Kavath**, a demi-plane of permanent twilight wrapped in the membrane called the Veil.

The sky: a permanent swirl of bruised purple, faintly luminous but providing no useful light. No sun. No moon. No stars. No weather.

The sea: gone. The harbor channels retained their water, but the “ocean” beyond the harbor mouth was replaced by the Veil, a shimmering, semi-permeable boundary visible from the outermost quays as a wall of iridescent nothing.

The inhabitants: alive. Terrified. Trapped. And, within weeks, in the dark.

#### The Darkness

The displacement was not gradual. One moment Gloamvere City existed under open sky in Arche with a functioning economy, fuel supply, and trade network. The next moment, all of that was gone. The city had whatever oil, tallow, and wood it had on hand when the sky changed. Street lamps burned. Fireplaces burned. Forge fires burned. Within weeks, the fuel ran out.

The darkness that followed was not metaphorical. Gloamvere City was lightless. People burned furniture. They burned cloth. They burned the doors off their own homes. And then they had nothing left to burn.

Food was a parallel crisis. Gloamvere had been a trading port, it imported the majority of its food supply. The displacement severed every trade route simultaneously. The city’s warehouses held perhaps a month of grain at normal consumption. Rationing extended that. The fishing fleet was useless, there was no ocean to fish. The underground aquifers still produced water, and the cisterns were full from the last rains before the Rite, so dehydration was not the immediate threat. Starvation was.

What saved the city from immediate collapse was the same quality that had defined it for four centuries: obstinate refusal to die. The Archeans had survived the Silver Bellies, the Pyre of Stone, and the Glass Rain. They had a cultural infrastructure of crisis response, guild hierarchies that knew how to organize labor, rationing systems that had been tested in previous calamities, and a population psychologically conditioned to treat catastrophe as a challenge rather than an ending.

The first generation’s governing body was an emergency council drawn from surviving guild leadership and noble families, the ancestor of the current factional structure, though it would not split into three for nearly a century.

#### The First Light

What the first generation discovered, and what changed everything, was the grey residue accumulating in the canal channels.

The displacement had saturated Kavath’s atmosphere with cognitive substance. Memory, uncontained and unprocessed, condensed into the canals the way dew collects on cold glass. The resulting sludge was useless, contextless, and faintly luminous.

Someone put a match to it. The sludge burned with a cold, dim, grey-violet glow that flickered with half-formed feelings. But it was light.

The first lamps were crude: clay dishes of canal sludge with cloth wicks. They smelled of ozone and metal. They radiated uninvited emotions. The light was ugly, unstable, and better than darkness. Every civilization has a first fire. This one burned on the residue of other people's lives.

(The full account of the lamp origin and its philosophical implications is covered in the Memory Economy & Technology document. What matters here is that the discovery of memory-as-fuel was the survival event that made everything else possible.)

#### Food and Survival

The food crisis was slower to resolve and never fully resolved in the Dark Years. The city’s survival depended on three developments:

**Subterranean cultivation.** The crypt-tunnels beneath the city, expanded during the Year of the Glass Rain, provided dark, humid environments where certain fungi and root crops could be grown. Archean alchemists adapted pre-displacement agricultural knowledge to underground cultivation, producing a calorie base that was nutritionally adequate and gastronomically grim. Gloamvere City’s cuisine to this day reflects this origin, fungal staples, root vegetables, and preserved proteins form the backbone of common diets.

**The Veil boundary.** The Veil is semi-permeable. Not to people, a person who walks into the Veil does not come back, but to certain material exchanges at the boundary layer. The condensation that forms on deep stone walls where the city meets the Veil contains trace minerals and nutrients. Over time, the Wellkeepers (who predate the factions) learned to cultivate this boundary chemistry, producing mineral-rich water supplements that filled critical gaps. It did not feed the city. It kept people alive long enough to build something that could.

**Dimensional travelers.** Kavath is not perfectly sealed. The Veil weakens at Thin Spots, and over decades, the first travelers from outside found their way in. They brought goods, food, seeds, and trade. Gloamvere City did not re-establish Arche’s global commerce, but it developed a trickle trade with planar travelers, adventurers, and beings from adjacent dimensions who found the city’s unique exports, bottled memories, preserved emotions, artifacts infused with human experience, valuable enough to warrant the trip.

This trickle trade is still Gloamvere City’s primary connection to the outside. It is neither reliable nor sufficient to sustain the city alone. Gloamvere City is fundamentally self-reliant, and the memory economy is both its internal industry and its export market.

**GM Use (Dark Years)**
- Scarcity is foundational trauma: the city learned “light is life” and “memory burns.”
- The first lamps are a perfect myth-object (holy, shameful, revolutionary, depending on faction).
- If you want horror, make early Drift side effects linger in old neighborhoods and bloodlines.

### The Kindling (~40–120 Post-Displacement)

#### The Discovery of Memory as Substance

The canal sludge burned. That was the Dark Years’ survival discovery. The Kindling was the era in which people began to ask *why*.

The first generation had been too focused on survival to investigate. The second generation, born in Gloamvere City, never having seen the sun, knowing Arche only through their parents’ increasingly hazy recollections, was the generation that experimented.

The key observations, arrived at gradually over decades:

**Memory was leaking.** The displacement had not just moved the city, it had changed the rules. Cognitive substance, the material of thought, memory, and experience, had become tangible in Kavath’s atmosphere. People were losing memories slowly, involuntarily, and the lost substance was condensing into the canals and the air. The first generation’s gradual forgetting of Arche was not just the natural fading of time. It was *physical*. Their memories were seeping out of them and pooling in the waterways.

**The leakage could be controlled.** Accidental discovery, likely by an early Conclave ancestor: a person who held a glass vessel near their head while concentrating intensely on a specific memory found that the vessel filled with faint, luminous vapor. The memory dimmed in their mind. The vapor glowed in the glass. Extraction was possible.

**The extracted substance had properties.** Different memories produced different vapors. Sensory memories were light and evaporated quickly. Skills and procedures were denser and more stable. Emotional memories were intense, volatile, and left anyone who inhaled the vapor briefly overwhelmed by feelings that weren’t their own. Full life experiences, extracted only from the willing or the desperate, were the most complex and the most valuable.

**The substance could be used.** Burned, it produced light (already known). Inhaled, it transmitted the memory to the consumer (new and terrifying). Infused into objects, it gave those objects properties related to the memory’s content (discovered accidentally by a craftsperson who spilled memory vapor onto a workpiece and found the tool afterward seemed to guide their hand).

These discoveries did not happen in a single laboratory or a single year. They accumulated over the span of eighty years, contributed to by hundreds of people across what was still nominally a unified city. But as the implications became clear, the unity began to fracture, because the discoveries didn’t just reveal what memory could *do*. They forced the city to decide what memory *was*.

#### The Proto-Factions

The three-way split that would become the Spiritus Conclave, the Old Blood Council, and the Siderian Clade did not emerge overnight. It crystallized over the Kindling era as different communities within Gloamvere City responded to the memory discoveries according to their existing values:

**The artisan guilds**, descendants of Arche’s weavers, smiths, carpenters, and builders, were the first to experiment with memory infusion. They were makers by nature, and when they discovered that memory could be worked into physical objects, they saw a new craft. Their instinct was to *create*. They became the Spiritus Conclave.

**The noble families**, descendants of Arche’s aristocratic and priestly houses, were the first to recognize the theological implications. If memory was leaking from people involuntarily, then the city was losing its history. The families with the means to do so began *collecting*, preserving the first generation’s memories of Arche before they degraded beyond recovery. Their instinct was to *protect*. They became the Old Blood Council.

**The natural philosophers**, descendants of Arche’s scholars, engineers, and physicians, were the first to ask *how*. While the artisans crafted and the nobles hoarded, the philosophers measured, categorized, and experimented. They built the first Eidolon Compasses. They mapped the degradation curve. They discovered that memory could be refined from lower tiers to higher. Their instinct was to *understand*. They became the Siderian Clade.

The names came later. The philosophies were present from the beginning.

#### The Lamp Schism, The First Political Crisis

The event that formally ended the unified governance of post-displacement Gloamvere City was not a war. It was an argument about lamps.

By the late Kindling era (~year 100), Gloamvere City’s lamp technology had advanced beyond crude canal sludge. Artisan-crafted lanterns burning curated memory produced better light. Preserved high-quality fuel produced stable light. Refined low-grade waste burned efficiently in mass-produced fixtures.

The three proto-factions each developed their own approach to civic lighting, and each approach implied a fundamentally different vision for what Gloamvere City should be:

The artisans wanted to craft lanterns infused with selected emotions, light that didn’t just illuminate but *felt* like something. Warm light for homes. Alert light for workshops. Welcoming light for public spaces. Their vision: a city that *cared* about the quality of its own atmosphere.

The noble families wanted to stop burning memory in lamps entirely. Every Volatilis burned as fuel was a fragment of someone’s experience destroyed. Their vision: a city that respected memory too much to use it as a consumable.

The philosophers wanted efficient, standardized lighting across the city, maximum illumination for minimum resource expenditure, with no emotional content because emotional content was a variable that introduced inefficiency. Their vision: a city that worked *well*.

None of them were wrong. All of them were incompatible.

The emergency council, which had governed by consensus for a century, could not resolve the dispute because the dispute was not about lamps. It was about the nature of memory and the purpose of Gloamvere City’s new economy. The council fragmented along the same lines that the discoveries had drawn, and within a generation, three separate governing bodies had established territorial authority over different districts.

The lamp schism did not produce violence. It produced *borders*. And borders, once drawn, created the political geography that Gloamvere has maintained for four hundred years.

**GM Use (Kindling)**
- Play this era as discovery horror: people realize identity is extractable and tradable.
- The Lamp Schism is a non-war origin of borders, perfect for intrigue and old legal claims.
- Seed proto-faction figures as Tinctura NPCs (inventors, ethicists, smugglers, hoarders).

### The Consolidation (~120–300 Post-Displacement)

#### Formalization

The Consolidation was two centuries of institution-building. The proto-factions became the factions. The informal territories became formal districts. The ad hoc memory trade became a structured economy with standardized currency tiers, quality assurance, and regulated markets.

Key developments:

**The Spiritus Conclave** formalized the guild structure that would define it, the apprentice-to-master progression (later refined into the Bound-to-Athanor hierarchy), the Loom-Spires as territorial home base, and the craft philosophy that memory is living spiritus deserving of a home. The Conclave established the phialwright trade, giving all three factions a shared dependency on Conclave-manufactured containers.

**The Old Blood Council** consolidated its vault holdings, formalized the Ashen Ancestors theology, and established the Silent Ossuary as both territorial headquarters and the largest memory preservation facility in Gloamvere City. The Living Reliquary practice, permanently consuming an ancestor’s Quintessa to carry their identity, was codified as a theological sacrament during this era. The Shroud-Bearers of Lahkrima emerged as the Old Blood’s compassionate public face, providing grief counseling and spiritual care to common people.

**The Siderian Clade** built the Glass Spine, established the research academy, and developed the construct program. The first Vigil-class constructs were deployed for border patrol around year 200, a technological leap that gave the Clade a security advantage the other factions have never fully matched. The Clade also built the city’s first pneumatic communication system, water filtration stations, and structural monitoring network during this era.

**The neutral guilds**, the Wellkeepers, the Bargemen, the Couriers, established their civic charters, formalizing their independence from all three factions. The Grand Rib-Market emerged as the central trade hub, governed by a neutral market authority that all three factions agreed to respect because none could afford to control trade alone.

**The currency was standardized.** The seven-tier hierarchy (Volatilis through Athanor) was codified during the mid-Consolidation, replacing an earlier patchwork of informal valuations. The naming convention has changed twice, from folk terminology (v1) to psychological terminology (v2) to the current alchemical naming (v3.0), but the tier structure has remained stable since its establishment.

**GM Use (Consolidation)**
- This is where laws harden: charters, borders, tariffs, contraband definitions, “authorized” extraction.
- Neutral guilds are power brokers, use permits, routes, and civic leverage as plot tools.
- When players ask “why can’t we just…?”, the answer often lives here: bureaucracy weaponized.

### The 500-Year Expansion

#### Principle

Five hundred years is enormous. Following real-world human expansion patterns, adjusted for Kavath’s unique constraints, Gloamvere would not remain a lone settlement. It became the capital of a broader settled region within Kavath.

#### Slowing Factors
- The Dark Years wiped out 40 years of potential exploration (pure survival mode)
- The memory wilderness is hostile, emotional hazards, memory-warped creatures, disorienting terrain
- No sun means no natural navigation aids, no seasonal agriculture cycles outside the city
- The factions spent the Consolidation era focused on internal politics rather than outward expansion
- The memory economy initially rewarded internal citizen extraction, not external harvesting

#### Accelerating Factors
- Resource pressure from a growing population in a closed city
- The Clade’s identity is built on understanding and expanding, they would fund expeditions
- Wild memory deposits in the wilderness have economic value
- Fragment arrivals forced contact whether Gloamvere wanted it or not
- 500 years is 10–20 generations of accumulated knowledge and population growth

#### Expansion Timeline Detail

**Dark Years (0–40 post-displacement):** Nobody leaves the city. The wilderness is unknown and terrifying. All energy goes to survival.

**Kindling (40–120):** Scouts venture past the city edge, initially motivated by desperation, looking for food sources, water, anything. They discover harvestable wild memory. Scavenger culture begins. First crude maps of immediate surroundings. A few camps established a day’s walk out. Most don’t last. Some do.

**Early Consolidation (120–200):** As factions formalize, so does expansion. The Clade sends organized survey expeditions. The Conclave sends artisans to establish workshops near rich wild-memory deposits where the spiritus “sings cleaner.” The Old Blood sends Reliquarians to investigate Aethos formations beyond the city’s influence. First permanent outposts, small, faction-affiliated, purpose-built. Research stations and harvest camps, not towns.

**Mid-Consolidation (200–300):** Outposts grow and merge. The first Fragment arrives, a chunk of farmland with terrified people materializes a week’s travel from Gloamvere City. This changes everything: Gloamvere City is no longer alone in Kavath. The factions argue about response, but contact happens regardless. Trade begins. More Fragments arrive. Gloamvere City’s outposts become waypoints connecting the city to Fragment settlements. Overland travel is formalized by the Wellkeepers or a new guild.

**Late Consolidation / Early Modern (300–400):** Gloamvere City is now the center of a network. A dozen or more settlements exist within reachable distance, some are Gloamvere outposts that grew into towns, some are Fragment communities that developed independently, some are hybrid. The closest are essentially suburbs. The further ones have their own governance, their own memory economy, their own culture. The Clade has mapped significant portions of the accessible surface. Substrate access points discovered at multiple locations beyond Gloamvere’s immediate reach.

**Modern Era (400–500+):** Present day. Gloamvere City is the largest, wealthiest, and most politically powerful settlement in known Kavath, but not the only one. It is the cultural and economic capital of a region that includes established towns, frontier outposts, Fragment communities in various stages of development, and uncharted wilderness beyond the mapped territory. Some outer settlements are loyal partners, some resentful tributaries, some fully independent. The factions extend influence outward, Conclave guild halls in allied towns, Clade research stations at distant substrate sites, Old Blood vault-chapels where they can establish bloodline authority, but their grip weakens with distance.

#### Post-Displacement Arche

When Gloamvere City vanished from Arche, it left a hole, literally. The lagoon islands are simply gone. A void in the water where a city used to be. The Vastus River now empties into a bay that shouldn’t exist. The surrounding region experienced economic collapse (their trade bottleneck disappeared overnight), followed by slow recovery over centuries.

Some communities near the former site still experience Veil bleedthrough, strange moods, memory distortions, a persistent sense of something missing. The dimensional scar left by the Rite of Unmaking has not fully healed after 500 years. This bleedthrough is one mechanism by which people in Arche occasionally stumble into Kavath through Thin Spots.

#### Gameplay Implications

- **Urban campaigns** in Gloamvere City proper (existing lore)
- **Frontier campaigns** in outer settlements and wilderness, different tone, threats, and politics
- **Exploration campaigns** in unmapped territory, deep wilderness, undiscovered Fragments, substrate delving
- **Diplomatic campaigns** between Gloamvere City and independent Fragment communities with their own agendas
- **Horror campaigns** in dead Fragments or deep substrate where the Aethos presence is strongest

**GM Use (Expansion)**
- Distance dilutes faction control: “Gloamvere rules” become negotiable the farther out you go.
- Fragments let you inject any genre pocket (pastoral horror, refugee enclave, fae polity, ruined experiment).
- Travel isn’t just geography, make routes emotionally hazardous (Drift weather, memory predators, intention storms).

### The Modern Era (~300–Present)

#### Five Centuries In

Modern Gloamvere City is a city that has forgotten it was once something else. The population has never seen sunlight. The ocean is a word in a theology lecture. Arche is not a memory, it is a story told by institutions that claim authority over stories.

The factions have been stable for roughly four hundred years. This does not mean they are static, the internal tensions documented in the Factions Reference are ongoing and genuine. But the three-way balance of power, the seven-district geography, and the memory economy’s basic structure have persisted because no faction can operate without the others.

The city’s relationship to its own history is defined by a paradox: Gloamvere City is built on memory, trades in memory, and fights over memory, but it has almost no memory of its own origin that exists outside the sealed vaults of one faction. The Old Blood’s deepest vaults contain Tinctura and Quintessa of the displacement generation, people who saw the sun, heard the ocean, felt the violet shockwave. These are the most valuable and most politically powerful artifacts in the city. They are also the least accessible. The Old Blood’s authority depends on possessing what no one else can verify.

The Hollow Flame’s central question, *why should this history belong to one faction?*, gains its weight from the five-century distance. When the Old Blood says “we preserve Arche’s memory,” they are not curating recent history. They are gatekeeping the foundational narrative of an entire civilization.

#### The Char Fire (~440–460 Post-Displacement)

Two generations before the present, a fire of disputed origin gutted a canal-side neighborhood between the Ossuary and the Dredge-Canals. The Old Blood calls it arson. The Clade calls it an industrial accident. The Hollow Flame calls it a “necessary illumination.”

The ruins were never rebuilt. The district became the Char, de facto territory for the Hollow Flame and others who exist outside formal power structures. The fire’s origin remains the most politically charged unsolved question in recent Gloamvere City history. People alive today had grandparents who watched it burn. Witness accounts still circulate, and contradict each other.

(Full details in the Geography & Districts document.)

#### The Veil in the Modern Era

Five hundred years of observation have not resolved the nature of the Veil. What has been established:

**Physical properties:** The Veil is a semi-permeable boundary enclosing Kavath. It is visible from Gloamvere City’s outermost districts as an iridescent shimmer at the edge of sight. It weakens at thresholds, doorframes, archways, open graves, empty rooms, and these Thin Spots are where planar travelers enter and exit, where Preservation rituals are performed, and where the Veil’s boundary effects are most pronounced.

**The whispers:** The sky whispers. This is not disputed. Every faction has documented it. What the whispers *are* is disputed. The Old Blood says regrets of the living. The Clade says auditory hallucinations from memory vapor. The occasional reports of the whispers forming words in an unknown language are filed, noted, and not discussed in polite company.

**Canal anomalies:** Downstream, where memory runoff concentrates, people occasionally report emotional impressions that don’t match any known human experience. These reports are rare, difficult to verify, and easy to dismiss as Drift symptoms. They persist across five centuries of documentation.

**The watching:** The Conclave’s oldest oral tradition. Not written down, not shared with outsiders, passed from master to apprentice for five hundred years: the spiritus sometimes behaves as though it is being observed. Not all the time. Not in any pattern the Conclave has identified. But occasionally, when working with memory at its most sensitive, during Consonance attempts, during high-tier infusion, during the moments when the boundary between artisan and material is thinnest, something *attends*.

None of this proves anything. All of it fits within mundane explanations. The ambiguity is the point.

**GM Use (Modern Era)**
- Use “history as contraband” as your evergreen conflict generator.
- The Char Fire is your “recent trauma” anchor, alive in living memory, politically usable, unsolved.
- Keep the Veil consistent in behavior, disputed in meaning.

### The Cult of the Fractured Mirror, What Remains

#### The Short Answer

Nothing. The Cult was destroyed by its own Rite. The cultists were annihilated at the point of casting. No survivors. No Tinctura. No Quintessa. No memory artifacts of any kind from the Marrow Vault at the moment of displacement. The Cult of the Fractured Mirror is gone.

#### The Longer Answer

The Cult is gone. The question, the question that no one in Gloamvere City can answer, that keeps theologians awake and researchers funded and conspiracy theorists in business, is whether what the Cult *served* is also gone.

Lady Nyxentia, whatever she was, wanted Arche broken. The city was broken. It was not destroyed, it was *displaced*. Deposited in a pocket dimension where human memory becomes extractable substance. Wrapped in a membrane that weakens at thresholds and whispers in an unknown language. Saturated with an atmosphere that slowly draws cognitive substance out of its inhabitants and pools it in canals and in the air.

If Nyxentia was a mortal scholar who discovered a planar weak point and miscalculated, this is all physics. The Veil is a natural demi-plane. The memory phenomenon is a property of the dimension. The whispers are vapor artifacts. The city adapts and endures, as it always has.

If Nyxentia was something else, something that wanted this outcome, that wanted a population of people slowly leaking their experiences into an environment where those experiences become harvestable, then the Cult didn’t fail. The Cult succeeded perfectly. The cultists were tools, and tools are discarded after use.

**What factions believe (public vs private):**

The Old Blood officially considers the Cult a historical evil, defeated by its own hubris, and Lady Nyxentia a mortal heretic. Unofficially, the deepest vaults contain Tinctura of the pre-displacement period that certain Exalted Scions have consumed and refused to discuss afterward. The phrase “she is still watching” appears in the private journals of three separate Living Reliquaries across a span of two centuries. The journals are sealed.

The Clade considers the Cult irrelevant to current operations. Lady Nyxentia’s nature is a theological question, and the Clade does not do theology. Unofficially, the Clade’s deep-Veil research team, the scientists who study the boundary layer itself rather than its contents, has a higher rate of mental health incidents than any other division. Three researchers across the past century have requested immediate transfer after prolonged Veil-boundary exposure. Their debriefs are classified. The term “pattern recognition in noise” appears in all three.

The Conclave does not discuss the Cult publicly. The oldest masters know the oral tradition. They know about the watching. They treat it not as a threat but as a *condition*, the way a fisherman treats the tide. You respect it. You work with it. You do not pretend it is not there. And you do not, under any circumstances, try to talk to it.

*(GM guidance: The Veil and Nyxentia are deliberately ambiguous narrative dials, see the GM Use blocks throughout this chapter.)*

**GM Use (Cult Remains)**
- The Cult is dead; the patron-question is alive. Keep that separation clean.
- Use sealed journals and classified debriefs as truth-fragments that always come with a price.
- If PCs try to “contact Nyxentia,” make it taboo across factions (different reasons, same fear).

### Potential Plot Hooks

#### For Arche History

**The Anchor:** A player character finds an old coin from Arche. It is warm to the touch, acting as a planar compass pulling the party toward a Thin Spot where Kavath can be traversed.

**The Rescue:** A descendant of an Archean merchant hires the party. They believe their great-grandfather is still alive in Gloamvere City, impossible, given the five-century gap, unless the Veil does something to time that hasn’t been documented.

**The Leak:** Veil bleedthrough intensifies in a coastal town of Arche, causing people to lose their will to live. The party must enter Kavath to plug the leak, but plugging it means understanding what the Veil *is*, and no one in Gloamvere City agrees.

#### For the Post-Displacement Eras

**The First Lamp:** A Tinctura surfaces on the black market that purportedly contains the memory of the person who first burned canal sludge for light, the foundational act of Gloamvere City’s entire economy. All three factions want it. The Old Blood wants it for the vault. The Conclave wants it as a craft relic. The Clade wants to analyze it. The Hollow Flame wants to release it to the public. The player decides who gets it, or whether to consume it themselves.

**The Lost Crypt:** Construction in the Dredge-Canals uncovers a sealed passage leading downward, toward the Marrow Vault, the crypt where the Rite was performed. The passage has been sealed since the displacement. All three factions want it explored. None want to send their own people first. The party is hired as expendable investigators. What they find depends on where the GM has set the Nyxentia dial.

**The Founders’ Schism:** An Old Blood Living Reliquary consumed a Quintessa of a Kindling-era ancestor and has been behaving strangely since, making public statements that contradict Old Blood theology, claiming the proto-factions split not over philosophical differences but because someone *told* them to split. Someone who spoke through the canal fog. The Old Blood wants the Reliquary contained. The Clade wants to interview them. The Conclave’s oldest master has been seen visiting the Reliquary in secret.

#### For the Nyxentia Question

**The Whisper Translation:** A Clade linguist claims to have decoded a fragment of the sky-whispers. The translation is a single sentence. Every faction interprets it differently. The sentence is: *"Thank you for building me a mouth."*

**The Anomalous Canal:** A section of the Dredge-Canals has begun producing emotional impressions that are not human, feelings with no human analogue, experienced by anyone who enters the water. The Clade wants to study it. The Old Blood wants it sealed. A Conclave master says, quietly, “She’s dreaming.”
